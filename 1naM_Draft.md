## The grid-shutdown timeline — pivotal subplot (new)

Full technical detail lives in `power-grid-shutdown-timelines.md` in the repo root — treat it as researched-but-speculative canon (its own confidence caveats apply) and use it for both hazard logic and regional flavor. Per the author, this needs **dedicated scenes**, not just background texture. Key mechanics to build from:

- **Fast collapse, not instant collapse.** Gas and coal generation fail within hours to ~5 days as unmonitored plants trip and fuel handling stops. Wind is non-hazardous and fails fast too. This is what powers the "just-in-time phone call" pressure above — real infrastructure (cell towers, landlines, data centers) rides on backup power for a finite, short window before it goes dark for good.
- **Nuclear is the real danger, and it's regional, not global, and it's slow.** Reactors SCRAM safely within hours (grid instability trips them, not internal failure), then run on emergency diesel/battery backup for the reactor core and spent fuel pool for roughly 3 days. Real danger window is **weeks 3-8 post-event**, when spent fuel pools that have lost active cooling can boil dry and expose fuel rods — a Fukushima-style regional contamination event with a years-to-decades exclusion zone, not an instant global threat.
- **Location matters and should drive decisions.** John's own home region (Alpharetta, GA) sits close to Plant Vogtle and in a state with one of the highest nuclear-generation shares of anywhere studied (34.7%) — among the worst-ranked of the locations profiled in the reference doc. Reno, NV (TruckHouse's location) has **zero** nuclear generation and strong solar/geothermal durability — the single safest grid profile studied. This gives the TruckHouse relocation a concrete, ticking-clock, life-safety reason (get away from Southeastern reactor country before the weeks 3-8 window) layered on top of the existing "self-sufficient mobile base" logic, rather than that logic standing alone.
- **Hydro is a wildcard, not a hazard by default** — dams can fail if a flood event hits with no one managing floodgates, but this is conditional and downstream-only, not a certainty.
- **Settled: John's home grid power fails around midday on Day 0** (roughly 12:00-1:00 PM Eastern), not within the first few hours. Reasoning: Georgia Power's Alpharetta-area mix is gas-dominant (38.6%, the largest single source), and per the reference doc that generation fails on a "hours to ~1-2 days" timeline as cascading grid instability trips unmonitored plants and pipeline compressor stations lose oversight — midday is a defensible point inside that window. This preserves Section 1 as written (John has full grid/wifi/laptop access all morning, through the 911 call and drive home, all before midday) with no rewrite needed. Cell/phone infrastructure runs on its own separate backup power and should be treated as surviving somewhat *longer* than the home grid — this is what still makes the "just-in-time phone call" to Sam plausible later that same day, even after John's own house has gone dark.
- **Naval Station Mayport** (if/when the story returns there later) sits on a fossil-fuel-dominant municipal grid (JEA) with no on-site reactor but indirect exposure via a Plant Vogtle power-purchase contract — worth remembering if that location comes back into play, but not yet load-bearing until a future section actually gets there.
- **Now load-bearing (Section 7): the Pacific Northwest's hydro-heavy grid is why Portland/Oregon still has working phone infrastructure** when Huntsville, AL and Phoenix, AZ do not. Consistent with this doc's existing hydro-resilience logic (same reasoning already used for Newfoundland in Section 6) — real-world basis: Washington and Oregon both generate a majority/large share of power from hydro. This is presented in Section 7 as John's own in-scene deduction, not omniscient narration.
- Per the "new explicit subplot" direction: look for real scenes built around this, not just narration — e.g., characters reasoning explicitly about which regions to avoid or move through, a supply run that has to route around a reactor's rough exclusion radius once the weeks-3-8 window is close, or a character with direct knowledge of a specific plant. Don't just mention it in passing.
