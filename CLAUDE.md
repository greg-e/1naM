# CLAUDE.md: 1 in a Million, Canon & Continuity Rules

This file is the working rulebook for the book and the source of truth for canon. If something isn't described here, it isn't canon. Prose lives in [sections/](sections/), one scene per file, named `NN-dayX-slug.md` (e.g. [sections/01-day0-john.md](sections/01-day0-john.md)).

- Before writing or editing a scene, skim the relevant character and plot entries below.
- New scenes go in new files under `sections/`, never inline in this file.
- **Keep this file current.** When canon changes, edit the relevant entries in place so the file always describes the current state; git history is the record of what changed. Propose any change to a settled beat or to the order of events to the author before making it.

## Core premise

- The event happens at 2:00 a.m. Eastern, September 5, 2028. Fast-acting, airborne, near-total in lethality.
- About 1 in a million survive: roughly 350 in the continental US, with no concentration anywhere. Survivor density is higher in Canada, South America, Europe, and Asia (exact ratios not yet set).
- Most survivors don't know they're immune until they wake into the silence. Death was instantaneous and symptom-free for almost everyone, with nothing to distinguish one death from another.
- **Why some people survive is a deliberate mystery**, and it's the emotional and thematic core of the title. Never resolve it: no genetic or hereditary theory, no antibody theory, no exposure-survivor case study. **What killed everyone else is a separate, answerable question**, and the Italy excursion researches it and may reach real findings. The line: the research can explain the killing mechanism, never the immunity.
- September 5, 2028 is a **Tuesday, the day after Labor Day**. Section 1 uses this (John rules out "must be a holiday" because he'd just had Monday off). It's the first normal work and school day after a long weekend, so no character has an "everyone's off" explanation.

## Cast

### John Lauer

**61, Alpharetta, GA** (a subdivision off Old Milton Parkway near the Georgia 400 interchange). Principal Applied AI Architect at Google, instrument-rated private pilot, widower.

**Appearance.** 6'1", 195 lbs, tall and lanky. Caucasian, with brown eyes and a clear, tanned, ruddy complexion. A thick brown beard with white patches and thick brown hair streaked with white and gray; in another era you could mistake him for Grizzly Adams or Jeremiah Johnson. Black Wayfarer-style glasses. Dresses in T-shirts, olive drab BDU cargo pants, and flip-flops, with a baseball cap outdoors.

**Temperament.** Panic and competence run side by side in him, a habit from the overnight grill line. In company he holds himself together through his own losses; alone, he doesn't always. Adversarial, red-team instincts: he looks for how a system can be made to do something it wasn't meant to.

**Background.**
- **Widowed.** His wife **Naomi** died sixteen years before the event. No children; his brother Mark is his only close family. He has kept **Naomi's study** unchanged (her books, a reading chair, a rolltop desk) and sometimes sits in it.
- **Principal Applied AI Architect, Public Sector, at Google** (D.C.-office-based, hybrid from metro Atlanta). A 30-year systems architect, telemetry specialist, and former federal security consultant who leads Gemini deployments for civilian infrastructure and transportation-safety agencies (DOT/FAA, DHS, USCG, NOAA, FEMA). He works daily in ADS-B, AIS, NextGen 911/CAD, Cospas-Sarsat, orbital TLE, and DOT traffic feeds, and that fluency drives how the search works. His frontier-model access and his willingness to strip the safety layer off one come from the same background.
- As a young man, a network-intrusion and vulnerability-research episode ended in a federal consulting offer instead of prosecution; that's the origin of his red-team habits.
- Worked the overnight grill line at a Waffle House off North Avenue to put himself through Georgia Tech (B.S. ECE; M.S. Systems Engineering, Johns Hopkins). That's why he can cook.
- **Active private pilot, ASEL, instrument-rated and current.** He flies IMC regularly out of PDK in a 182, reads a FlightAware/ADS-B track fluently, knows what a deliberate diversion looks like, and knows an augmented-crew widebody carries three pilots. His ease on the radio comes from years of talking to ATC.

**In the story.**
- His Day 0 low point is a private cry to God, "the first sound like it since Naomi died," which opens his spiritual arc. **In Section 4 he takes Naomi's Bible off her desk**; carry it forward through that arc.
- His composure first breaks in front of another person over Tyler's grief in Section 6. This is a permanent turning point.
- **From Section 5, Sam is checking him out on the PC-12 in flight**, a turbine transition for a current piston IFR pilot. An ongoing mentorship arc; keep developing it.

## Places

### Port Angeles and the Hook

- **Grace's house** (217 Wapiti Way) is inland, ~7 mi south of the Strait shoreline and ~9 mi SW of downtown, in forested foothills toward Olympic National Park. **It never appears on-page.** The real-world pin is a vacation-rental business; don't use its name, and fictionalize the address before publication.
- **Arrival airport: KCLM** (William R. Fairchild International), non-towered `[verify]`. A rental car lot gets the group inland `[verify a rental presence]`.
- **Hospital: Olympic Medical Center**, Port Angeles, with its own backup generator plant `[verify facility details before quoting]`. The group uses it for Grace's acute treatment only and doesn't linger among its ~100–200 dead.
- **Interim lodging:** a cleared corner of a Port Angeles hotel or motel, between Grace's rescue and the station and cutter being ready. Play it as a small, partial version of the clearing at the Hook.
- **The Coast Guard Air Station/Sector Field Office on Ediz Hook** (48.1408°N, 123.4108°W) has a hangar for three MH-65E helicopters, an exchange, a medical/dental clinic, a pier for vessels up to 400 ft, a 41-ft utility boat, the patrol boats *Adelie* and *Cuttyhunk*, a 170-ft VTS radar tower and radio station, and its own airfield (**KNOW**, runway 8/26, 4,500×150 ft, ample for the PC-12; KCLM ~5 mi west is the alternate). There's no on-base housing; crews normally rent in town.
- **The cutter *Active*, where the group lives.** An Argus-class Offshore Patrol Cutter carrying the name of the 1966 Reliance-class WMEC-618 *Active* she replaced; the 2028 hull assignment is an authorial choice. She is ~360 ft (fits the pier), with a reverse-osmosis water plant, a large freezer and provisions for 60+ day patrols, modern generators, real berthing, a flight deck, and **her own medical bay**, where Grace's ongoing care happens. She was provisioned for an extended Alaska/Bering Sea fisheries patrol when the event hit. The group uses *Active* for berths, galley, power, water, and the freezer, and the station ashore for the clinic, hangar, airfield, and radios.
- **The dead at the Hook: 22.** The station's **17** (its 24-hour duty, watch, and alert crews; Grace's coworkers) and **5** aboard *Active* (a small duty section; most of her ~100–120 crew were at home). The group bags them and moves them to **the lumber buildings at the entrance to the Hook** (mill and log-yard structures at the landward end of the spit). They do the identification and ledger work and leave the bodies there, with no burial; the final disposition stays unresolved. This work, plus getting the station's radios running, fills the week between the group's arrival (~Day 2–3) and the ship's anchoring (≈Day 9–10).
- **Why the Hook, beyond its facilities:** clearing 22 known dead makes it a bounded haven, a finished piece of grief. The alternative is living for months among a county's dead (~100,000+ across Port Angeles, Sequim, and Clallam County) decomposing in homes, hotels, and campgrounds with nobody to tend them. Write to that contrast between bounded and unbounded horror on the page.
- **The *Pacific Tender*'s anchorage:** Port Angeles Harbor, inside Ediz Hook, ≈48.1300°N, 123.4170°W, mid-harbor, south of the pilot station, outside the 33 CFR 110.230 non-anchorage box and the log booming grounds, ~0.65 nm from the station by boat. The ship stays abandoned at anchor, not used as a base; her fate and her 22 dead are unresolved (`port-angeles-base-research.md` §8.9–8.10).
- **The stay lasts at least a couple of months**, through early to mid-November, long enough for Grace to start healing.
- Research and sourcing: `port-angeles-base-research.md`.

### TruckHouse (Reno/Sparks, NV)

Catherine's brother's overland-vehicle shop. The group goes there after the Port Angeles stay, so ~mid-November or later, and Reno sits at ~4,500 ft with Sierra winter a real factor. Two reasons drive the trip: Catherine wants to know what happened to her brother, and the group needs four of the shop's BCRs, self-contained campers, for the Italy excursion. ## Scene-by-scene canon

### Section 1: John's Day 0 morning

Covered by the Timeline and John's entry. John's first 911 call, answered by the AI triage agent, is what later seeds the nationwide search. His private breakdown, alone with the dog, is a cry to God.

### Section 2: Sam's flight

- **Delta 27** (DAL27/DL27), KATL to RKSI/ICN, **Airbus A350-900**, an ultra-long-haul sector (~15h20m block) with an augmented **three-pilot** crew: Sam (Captain), Theresa Foreman (First Officer), Jack Sommers (Relief Pilot). The reference dossier, `reference/DAL27_Emergency_Turnback_Dossier.pdf`, describes a four-pilot crew and a single pilot's medical incapacitation; use it for ATC, telemetry, and timing mechanics only.
- Sam is the only survivor on the flight deck. Theresa and Jack die at 2:00 at the controls while Sam rests.
- **Pushback 20:10 EDT Sept 4, wheels up ~20:25.** Jack takes the first rest block after top of climb and relieves Sam in the left seat at 00:30. Sam rests ~00:30–03:00, comes down for his handoff, finds Theresa and Jack dead, checks the cabin (a full load: **306 passengers**, every seat of Delta's 306-seat A350-900 layout, plus **13 cabin crew**, all dead; 322 aboard counting the three pilots), and **turns back at 03:30**, ~7 hours airborne, over the western North Slope (~69.5°N 160.5°W, ~3,240 nm from KATL). The ATL–ICN great circle runs Great Lakes, Manitoba/Saskatchewan, Mackenzie Delta, northern Alaska, Bering. Initial course home ~090° true. At 02:00 the aircraft is ~2,500 nm out, near the Mackenzie Delta.
- **Turning for Atlanta is a choice.** Kotzebue (~170 nm), Deadhorse (~250 nm), Nome (~325 nm), and Fairbanks (~410 nm, the natural ETOPS alternate with a long runway) are all closer. Nobody aboard can be saved by speed, and his family is in Marietta.
- At the turn he's well over max landing weight (A350-900 MLW ~207 t `[verify variant]`). Seven hours of normal cruise burns him under MLW with normal reserves; no jettison or holding. Sunrise comes over Manitoba near Lake Winnipeg, ~07:40, off the left side of the nose. He calls Anchorage, Edmonton, Winnipeg, Minneapolis, Chicago/Indianapolis, and Atlanta Center on the way home and hears nothing. He hand-flies a daylight visual approach to Runway 26R at KATL, ~10:30. His own empty airport disorients him more than a strange one would.
- **Inbound to Atlanta, Sam keys Guard (121.5)** as he has every twenty minutes since the North Slope, and this time John answers from Signature's ops desk. Sam knows Signature from his charter years and brings the aircraft there, to the GA side of the field, the part of KATL he can reach without a marshaller. **Section 2 ends as he taxis in**, radio contact made, before they've seen each other.
- Everyone aboard dies the same way as everyone else. Survival stays location-independent and unexplained.

### Section 3: the ramp meeting, Marietta, and the drive home

- **John sees Delta 27's track still moving** mid-morning after finding Harry, squawking 7700 and inbound to Atlanta. He drives to KATL, parks on the Signature ramp, walks through the unlocked lobby doors to the ops-desk radio, and scans frequencies until he raises Sam on Guard while Sam is still airborne. This pays off the book's "I want a name, a number, a voice" theme literally. Sam learns John's name on the radio; John learns Sam's at the handshake.
- **Sam lands ~10:30** and taxis to Signature. They meet on the ramp **~11:00**. Sam rides the emergency slide down; Dan reaches him first.
- They drive to Sam's house together (~11:30–12:35). **Sam finds Elena and Maya himself.** John stays at the threshold and lets him have the moment alone, the way John found Harry alone that morning. This is the first time the book's private-versus-witnessed grief distinction applies to Sam.
- They drive back to John's, arriving **~13:40**. The section ends on "Let's go find out how big this thing actually is."

### Section 4: John's house, the telemetry sweep, and seeding the nationwide search

- **John runs the DOT-camera/AIS/beacon/grid sweep with Sam watching**, so they work out the scope of the event together. The workstation's safety layer is intact for this; it runs on John's existing telemetry clearances.
- **The grid forecast:** the model runs the collapse forward, and John's Georgia dot comes back a bad color with a date on it, witnessed by Sam. Later that evening John walks Sam through Vogtle, the two reactors within ~80 miles, the scram-then-spent-fuel-pool mechanism, and the 10–21-day number, and says "we need to head west" for the first time.
- **The 911 realization.** John's own 911 call was answered by an AI agent with no human in the loop, so the agent answered *everyone*, and every survivor who called left the same kind of trace.
- **The safety layer comes off exactly once, live, in front of Sam.** Reaching county and regional dispatch systems means going where John has no authorization. He says the real weight of it first ("I've never actually done it for real"), and Sam's "Some decisions don't need a vote" tips the decision. The freed local model, coupled to frontier Gemini over the API, runs the nationwide 911 search.
- **Naomi's Bible:** John takes it from her desk. They're going to PDK deliberately the next day, not fleeing tonight.
- The search is seeded and left running overnight. Both men sleep at John's house. The grid goes dark ~00:50 and the generator picks up the load without a flicker, paying off John's earlier "I'm going to go double-check the generator before I turn in."

### Section 5: the three leads, the PC-12 PRO, and the launch

- **Three leads.** The search filters for callers who **pushed back** against the agent: ~4,000 pattern-matching calls yield **three hits with address and callback number: Huntsville AL, Phoenix AZ, and Elwha WA.** All three callbacks fail with a flat network-failure notice, not a ring; every region's network infrastructure is down within about a day. No voice, no name, no promise. Each lead is an address, a dead number, and a fragment of transcript proving someone was alive and fighting less than two days ago. The mission is to go stand on three points on a map with no idea whether anyone is still there.
- **Sam raises, unprompted, that the A350 is useless for this.** It's too big to reach a handful of survivors, so they drive to PDK.
- **The PC-12 PRO:** a brand-new demo aircraft behind the Pilatus dealer's glass, the service-door lockbox open in under a minute, fuel already topped off. "Not everything gets to be hard."
- **"You fly?":** John says he's instrument-current in a 182 with no turbine time, and Sam says he'll check him out as they go.

### The grid-shutdown subplot

Technical detail: `reference/power-grid-shutdown-timelines.md` (researched but speculative, with its own confidence caveats). This is a subplot, not background texture.

- **North America runs on four separately synchronized interconnections** (Eastern, Western, ERCOT, Quebec), and each fails on its own clock. The first ~2 hours are an automated silent window: streetlights, card-reading gas pumps, and municipal water pressure all still work. From hour ~2, thermal plants (coal, then gas) trip one at a time as fuel and fault limits catch up. Then frequency sags past the relay threshold and the whole interconnection cascades down within minutes. **Full lights-out for a region comes at hours 8–24.**
- **Hydro goes down with its interconnection** like everything else, through the same protective relaying. Power more than a day out comes only from:
  - **a small accidental pocket** where generation and load happen to balance, as in Huntsville (Section 6); small and temporary;
  - **a building's own backup generator** with days of fuel, like Olympic Medical Center's; hospitals and certified airports are required to have one;
  - **a property's own islanding-capable solar+battery system.**
- **Nuclear plants SCRAM safely within hours**, then run on backup diesel and batteries for ~3 days. The danger window is **~10–21 days after the trip**, when an uncooled spent fuel pool can boil dry and expose fuel rods: a regional, Fukushima-scale contamination event with a years-to-decades exclusion zone.
- **Location matters for nuclear exposure**, not for whose grid lasts longest. John's region (Alpharetta / Plant Vogtle) has the highest nuclear share and second-closest reactor of the locations profiled, the worst on the list. Reno and Port Angeles are both very low risk `[derived; general meteorology]`: the only Pacific Northwest plant, Columbia Generating Station, is ~220 mi east across the Cascades, and prevailing winds carry any release away. Verify Naval Base Kitsap's naval reactors (passive decay-heat removal, not the spent-fuel-pool scenario) and Hanford's legacy waste tanks before putting this on the page.
- **Power at the group's own base is never jeopardy.** The subplot's pressure is a date on a calendar. Any in-house power beat follows the Section 4 model: preparedness confirmed working. Don't write a backup failure or outage-driven tension unless the author asks.
- **Build real scenes on this**: characters reasoning about which regions to avoid, a supply run routed around a danger window, a character with direct plant knowledge.

## Writing rules

### Scarcity

With ~350 survivors nationwide, every search for survivors comes back sparse, never a flood of hits. Characters never know the true survivor count and don't act as if they do. Don't invent discovery mechanisms that make finding people easy; every survivor found costs real effort.

### Craft rules

- **Don't launch with known holes.** When the group plans a real-world logistics operation, show them catching and solving the gap before departure.
- **Verify before committing.** A low pass or visual check before landing anywhere without a working tower fits Sam's competence.
- **Keep the full cast engaged.** As the roster grows, find real tasks and scenes for supporting characters instead of letting the same few carry every beat.
- **Day numbering is relative.** Chapter order matters more than exact "Day N" labels. Use the Timeline for sequence and clock times.
- **Check who is actually present at 2:00 a.m. before placing a body.** Offices and schools are empty at that hour except for night shifts (security, hospital staff, 24-hour operations). A 24-hour place (a Waffle House, a hospital, a gas station) should have staff there.
- **Ground vehicles aren't a source of friction this early.** Batteries haven't died, gas hasn't gone stale, tires haven't gone flat. When the group needs a car, the answer is a **rental car lot**: keys in the consoles, tanks topped off by routine servicing, starts on the first try. Aircraft keep their real fuel, key, and maintenance friction, and vehicles many weeks or months on may not be so easy.
- **The "machine keeps going after the operator dies" motif** (Sam's A350, Johan's ship, Grace's crash) is used deliberately; any further use must be a conscious echo.

### Operational realism

Write every scene, new or revised, with procedural accuracy, correct domain terminology, and believable friction instead of dramatic abstraction.

**1. Concrete specificity over abstract drama.** Don't carry an outcome or feeling with generic emotional adjectives ("he was panicked," "it was intense"). Show the mechanical steps, equipment, control inputs, and conditions that produce the situation or answer it. Name the hardware, switches, sub-assemblies, and readings with their correct designations.

| Abstract (avoid) | Operationally realistic (target) |
| --- | --- |
| "He checked his gauges as the engine sputtered." | "He scanned the fuel flow indicator; port main was fluctuating below 120 lbs/hr despite the auxiliary pump remaining energized." |
| "The ship turned hard to dodge the submarine." | "He ordered hard right rudder, ringing down emergency full on the engine order telegraph to bite into the turn before the wake straightened." |
| "The helicopter flew through bad weather over the mountains." | "The aircraft held 4,500 ft MSL in light continuous turbulence, tracking the 040° radial to clear the ridgeline under visual flight rules." |

**2. Time, geometry, and friction.**
- *Procedural lag:* account for radio latency, distance, mechanical response (spool-up, hydraulic pressure build), checklist length, and fatigue errors.
- *Environmental limits:* line of sight, fuel endurance, weather minimums, thermal limits, weight and balance, communication dead zones.
- *Mental load:* filter decisions through exhaustion, sensory overload, and tunnel vision, never perfect omniscience.

**3. Professional cadence.** Professionals under stress use brevity codes, acronyms, and short direct syntax, not expository dialogue. Let the action make the jargon clear; no dictionary-style definitions.

**Technical ruleset:**
1. **Equipment:** precise makes, models, variants, and subsystems; correct military, maritime, aviation, and medical nomenclature.
2. **Procedure:** actions in the right order (checklist, directive, feedback, acknowledgment), with mechanical and biological delay.
3. **Logistics:** track finite resources (fuel in lbs/gal/endurance, battery capacity, thermal limits, supplies) and respect performance envelopes (density altitude, sea state, visibility).
4. **Perspective:** a character knows only what instruments, line of sight, or incoming comms tell them.

**Audit pass:** before finishing any prose, search for "quickly," "suddenly," "dangerous," "massive," "intense," and similar words, and replace them with measurements, instrument readings, or procedural steps.

**How this fits the rest of this file:**
- **Specifics must be real.** The PC-12 PRO, A350-900, MH-65E, C-17, Argus-class OPC, container ship, and ER and orthopedic procedures all have real specs. Check them against `reference/` or research, and flag anything unverified with `[verify]` rather than inventing a number. A confident wrong figure is worse than a vague one.
- **Canon still wins.** Added precision never overrides a settled beat or the order of events. It can move a clock time in the Timeline when the real-world math says it's wrong.
- **Not every scene needs friction.** Deliberately easy beats (the PDK lockbox, the Amarillo stop, the Phoenix→KCLM leg, rental cars) get the same concrete detail without manufactured trouble.
- **Grief beats stay.** The ban on emotional adjectives applies to them too: carry John's, Sam's, Catherine's, and Grace's grief through physical action and observed detail, but keep the emotional core.
- **Character descriptions are reference, not copy.** Use the appearance and habit details in the Cast entries through observed action on the page; don't paste them in as descriptive blocks.
- Write units as plain text in prose ("120 lbs/hr," "FL280," "121.5"), not LaTeX.
