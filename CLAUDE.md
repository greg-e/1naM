# CLAUDE.md — 1 in a Million: Canon & Continuity Rules

This file is the working rulebook for revising this book. Each narrative scene lives in its own file under [sections/](sections/) (named `NN-dayX-slug.md`, e.g. [sections/01-day0-john.md](sections/01-day0-john.md)) — that's the source of truth for prose. [1naM_Draft.md](1naM_Draft.md) is the index into those files plus the "CONTINUITY & PRODUCTION NOTES" section at its end, which is the full source of truth for canon and retcon history — this CLAUDE.md file distills the rules that must hold true in any new or revised scene. When the two disagree, treat the *end* of that section's retcon log as most current, since decisions were made one-by-one with the author over time and later entries supersede earlier ones.

Before writing or editing any scene, skim the relevant character/plot entries in the Continuity & Production Notes. Before a large structural change, propose it and get author confirmation the same way past retcons were logged — don't silently overwrite settled canon. When you write a new scene, create a new file in `sections/` and add it to the Table of Contents in 1naM_Draft.md — don't write narrative prose inline in 1naM_Draft.md itself.

## Core premise (fixed)

- The event happens at 2:00 AM Eastern, September 5, 2028. Fast-acting, airborne, near-total in lethality.
- Survival rate is ~1 in a million — roughly 350 survivors in the continental US, no concentration in any one place. Higher survivor density in Canada, South America, Europe, and Asia (exact ratios not yet pinned down).
- Most survivors don't know they're immune until they wake into the silence. Death was instantaneous and symptom-free for almost everyone — no illness, no warning, nothing to distinguish one death from another.
- **Why some people survive is a deliberate mystery.** This is the emotional and thematic core of the title. Do not resolve it. No genetic/hereditary theory, no antibody theory, no exposure-survivor case study.
- September 5, 2028 is a **Tuesday, the day after Labor Day** (Monday the 4th) — deliberate, already used in Section 1 (John rules out "must be a holiday" because he'd just had Monday off). Keep this in mind in later sections: it's the first normal workday/school day after a long weekend, so there's no plausible "everyone's off" explanation available to any character.

## The grid-shutdown timeline — pivotal subplot

Full technical detail lives in `power-grid-shutdown-timelines.md` (repo root) — researched-but-speculative, its own confidence caveats apply. This is now a **dedicated subplot**, not just background realism. Key mechanics:

- Gas/coal/wind generation fail within hours to ~5 days as unmonitored plants trip and fuel handling stops — this is what puts a real, short clock on phone/cell/data infrastructure staying alive at all (see the Sam discovery mechanism above; Section 3 dramatizes this directly — John's home workstation dies at midday and he has to relocate to a generator-powered school to keep working).
- Nuclear plants SCRAM safely within hours, then run on backup power for ~3 days. The real danger window is **weeks 3-8 post-event**, when an uncooled spent fuel pool can boil dry and expose fuel rods — a regional (not global), Fukushima-scale contamination event with a years-to-decades exclusion zone.
- **Location matters and should drive character decisions.** John's home region (Alpharetta, GA / Plant Vogtle) is one of the worst-profiled locations in the reference doc. Reno, NV (TruckHouse's location) has zero nuclear generation and the strongest studied grid durability. This gives the eventual TruckHouse relocation a real, ticking-clock safety reason, not just a "mobile self-sufficient base" rationale standing alone.
- **Settled: John's home grid power fails around midday on Day 0** (~12:00-1:00 PM Eastern) — not within the first few hours. This sits inside the reference doc's "hours to ~1-2 days" collapse window for gas-dominant grids like Alpharetta's, and preserves Section 1 as written (grid/wifi/laptop access all morning). Cell/phone infrastructure runs on separate backup power and should read as surviving *longer* than the home grid — that gap is what makes the just-in-time call to Sam later that day plausible.
- **Now load-bearing (Section 7): the Pacific Northwest's hydro-heavy grid is why Portland/Oregon still has working phone infrastructure** when Huntsville, AL and Phoenix, AZ do not, by Wednesday. Same hydro-resilience logic already used for Newfoundland in Section 6 — real-world basis: Washington and Oregon both generate a large share of power from hydro.

## Craft rules (how to write this book, not just what happens in it)

- **Don't launch with known holes.** When the group plans a real-world logistics operation, show them catching and solving the gap *before* departure, not glossing over it.
- **Verify before committing, on the ground and in the air.** A low pass / visual check before landing anywhere without a working tower fits Sam's established competence.
- **Keep the full cast engaged.** As the survivor roster grows, look for real tasks and scenes for supporting characters rather than letting the same few carry every beat.
- **Day-numbering is relative, not exact.** Internal chapter order matters more than every "Day N" label being perfectly synced. Keep relative order correct; don't force exact day-count consistency.
- **Check who'd actually be present at 2:00 AM before placing a body somewhere.** The event hits at 2:00 AM sharp — a specific hour, not "sometime during the day." Workplaces, schools, offices are realistically empty at that hour except for actual night shifts (security, hospital staff, 24-hour operations); don't populate a building with casualties just because it's normally staffed during the day. Caught once already in Section 3 (a school wrongly had a dead secretary in it — fixed to an empty, never-opened building instead). **Cuts both ways:** a genuinely 24-hour location (a Waffle House, a hospital, a gas station) *should* have staff present at 2:00 AM — Section 11 handled this correctly (Waffle House employees confirmed dead, but outside on a break, which is why the interior itself is bodiless).
- **Ground vehicles are not a source of friction, this early in the timeline.** It's only been a few days since the event — batteries haven't died, gas hasn't gone stale, tires haven't gone flat. The reliable, repeatable answer whenever the group needs a car is a **rental car lot**: keys already in the consoles, tanks topped off by routine pre-event servicing, starts clean on the first try. Established in Section 11 (Huntsville) specifically to replace an earlier draft that had invented unneeded battery/ignition trouble. Use this same beat anywhere the group needs a car for the foreseeable future — don't manufacture car trouble as a plot obstacle. (This doesn't apply to aircraft, which keep their own real fuel/keys/maintenance friction, or to vehicles many weeks/months further into the timeline, if the story gets there.)
- **Log retcons instead of silently overwriting.** When a change contradicts existing material, identify every affected section explicitly, confirm the decision with the author, and note what still needs cleanup.
- Treat the deleted post-Section-2 plot as gone, not as a well of names/beats to casually reuse — if something from it turns out to still be wanted, that's the author's call to bring back explicitly.
