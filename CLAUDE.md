# CLAUDE.md — 1 in a Million: Canon & Continuity Rules

This file is the working rulebook for revising [1naM_Draft.md](1naM_Draft.md). The draft's own "STORY BIBLE & PRODUCTION NOTES" section (bottom of that file) is the full source of truth, including retcon history — this file distills the rules that must hold true in any new or revised scene. When the two disagree, treat the *end* of the story bible's retcon log as most current, since decisions were made one-by-one with the author over time and later entries supersede earlier ones.

Before writing or editing any scene, skim the relevant character/plot entries in the story bible. Before a large structural change, propose it and get author confirmation the same way past retcons were logged — don't silently overwrite settled canon.

## Core premise (fixed)

- The event happens at 2:00 AM Eastern, September 5, 2028. Fast-acting, airborne, near-total in lethality.
- Survival rate is ~1 in a million — roughly 350 survivors in the continental US, no concentration in any one place. Higher survivor density in Canada, South America, Europe, and Asia (exact ratios not yet pinned down).
- Most survivors don't know they're immune until they wake into the silence. Death was instantaneous and symptom-free for almost everyone — no illness, no warning, nothing to distinguish one death from another.
- **Why some people survive is a deliberate mystery.** This is the emotional and thematic core of the title. Do not resolve it. No genetic/hereditary theory, no antibody theory, no exposure-survivor case study. This was explicitly retconned out once already — do not reintroduce without the author asking for it directly.
- The pathogen's environmental viral load decays over time in open air. By ~3 weeks post-event, ambient concentration is low enough that re-entry/re-exposure risk is minimal with standard decon. Before that threshold, exposure is still lethal (this is what kills the Soyuz crew at Day 10 — see below).

## Scarcity is a hard constraint

With ~350 survivors nationwide, any search for survivors must read as genuinely sparse — not a flood of hits. Rules of the search mechanic:

- 911 calls are answered by an automated AI agent, not silence. The search method is reading agent transcripts for callers who pushed back against the agent's reassurance, not "finding unanswered calls."
- **Tyler**, **Catherine**, and **Grace** were found via the 911 search.
- Characters never know the true survivor count and should not act like they do. The data they find should be mostly nothing, with occasional isolated blips that don't pan out, and only rare real signals.
- Don't casually invent new discovery mechanisms that make finding people easy. Every survivor found should cost the characters real effort (a filter refinement, a coincidence like an aircraft flyover, a beacon that took days to notice).

## Base of operations

- **TruckHouse** (overland-vehicle manufacturer outside Reno/Sparks, NV) is the group's original base. Their strategy, decided collectively Day 1: fixed structures are a single point of failure; TruckHouse's BCR (expedition vehicle) fleet is fully self-contained (solar, water, power) and mobile, so the group organizes around movable, self-sufficient vehicles rather than settling into one building. Food/water are treated as effectively abundant (shelf-stable supply sitting in every grocery store); the real constraint is shelter and power independence.
- The group later relocates to **Naval Station Mayport, FL**, staged for the ISS recovery operation, and lives aboard **AKULA** (a real-world-style expedition yacht used as the recovery vessel and, later, primary living quarters).
- **No second base.** Pelican Cove and any group split across two locations has been cut. The group stays together at Mayport/AKULA.
- **No Silver Creek Lodge arc** as a long-term base, and **no C-17-based airlift** to move there — see Fleet rule.

## Fleet rule — PC-12 only

Sam flies a **Pilatus PC-12 and nothing else** for the entire story. King Air, Gulfstream G650, Phenom 300, Citation, and the C-17 Globemaster have all been removed from canon. Rules that follow from this:

- No character in the group is a qualified helicopter or heavy-transport pilot, and no one acquires that qualification off-page. Don't solve a logistics problem by having someone suddenly fly something they haven't been established as qualified for.
- Long international legs (e.g., the Milan trip) must be broken into realistic multi-leg routes matching the PC-12's real range (~1,800 nm per leg), with rest stops — not one heroic nonstop flight.
- If the Malpensa-style crash-landing beat is reused, it now totals their actual primary aircraft (raising real stakes), not a rented luxury jet.
- Cruise speeds and range in prose must match the PC-12's real performance (~270 kt cruise) — don't let old drafts' "600+ mph" language stand uncorrected.

## The ISS / Soyuz thread (finalized fate — do not re-open as "unresolved")

- All 9 original ISS crew are established as alive pre-contact.
- **10 days after the event**, with zero contact from Earth, 4 of the 9 crew independently decided to attempt return via Soyuz rather than wait indefinitely. The capsule and landing worked exactly as designed — but the pathogen was still fully active at Day 10 (nowhere near the ~3-week decay threshold). All 4 died on/shortly after opening the hatch.
- This means **by the time Sam first makes contact with Voss (~Day 16–17), the Soyuz 4 are already dead.** This is not a future rescue mission to plan — it already happened before the group's involvement. Voss reveals this in the very first contact scene; she has been alone with that grief for about a week already.
- Remaining crew at first contact: **5, all Dragon-manifested, including Voss.**
- **There is no Kazakhstan rescue mission in this story.** It has been cut entirely — do not write a scene where the group plans, prepares for, or departs on a Soyuz-crew recovery trip. Any earlier "John promises to solve Kazakhstan" material is superseded and should not be treated as an active plot thread.
- The Dragon crew's recovery (splashdown, AKULA as recovery vessel) is real, does happen, and does **not** depend on the C-17 or any cut aircraft — AKULA was always the recovery vessel regardless of airlift questions.
- Any future JSC/Mission Control scene is intended to be **written fresh from scratch**, not patched from the old draft — the existing Building 30/CAPCOM material in the draft is a placeholder the author plans to replace entirely.

## Craft rules (how to write this book, not just what happens in it)

- **Don't launch with known holes.** Established pattern: when the group plans a real-world logistics operation (fuel range, unverified airport/ground conditions, crew fatigue), the story should show them catching and solving the gap *before* departure, not glossing over it. Treat this as a recurring value of the characters, not just a one-off beat.
- **Verify before committing, on the ground and in the air.** Sam's discipline of a low pass / visual check before landing anywhere without a working tower is an established character trait — keep using it when a scene calls for landing somewhere unconfirmed.
- **Keep the full cast engaged.** As the survivor roster grows, actively look for real tasks and scenes for supporting characters rather than letting the same few characters carry every beat.
- **Day-numbering is relative, not exact.** The author has confirmed that internal chapter order matters more than every "Day N" label being perfectly synced across the whole manuscript. Don't spend effort forcing exact day-count consistency unless asked; do keep the *relative order* of events correct.
- **Log retcons instead of silently overwriting.** When the author asks for a change that contradicts existing chapters, treat it the way past retcons were handled: identify every affected section explicitly, confirm the decision, and note what still needs cleanup rather than assuming a single edit fully propagates.
- Don't reintroduce cut characters, cut vehicles, or the genetic-survival theory as a shortcut to solve a plot problem — these were deliberate author decisions, not oversights.
