# Nuclear risk on John's first evening: cooling dependencies, evidence, and relocation

**Research baseline: 7 November 2026. Story: 5 September 2028.** Public-level civilian hazard research, not instructions to operate, enter, or interfere with a facility. No current plant-monitoring assessment or prediction of a real accident. Later design changes, actual 2028 plant states and available feeds remain fictional assumptions requiring author review.

## 1. Answer for the story

John can learn enough from preserved public documents to recognize a **credible, potentially serious unattended-cooling problem**, distinguish reactor designs, and prepare to leave. Those documents do **not** establish that Alpharetta becomes unsafe on a particular date, that every nearby plant fails, or that all pools uncover after the same interval.

His first-evening conclusion can reasonably be: shutdown buys safety but does not end cooling needs; normal recovery plans assume people; he cannot verify crucial conditions; and waiting may reduce his transport and information options. **Nuclear uncertainty supports preparation and can contribute to precautionary relocation. Alone, the presently supported evidence does not compel immediate flight from the house that evening.** This is decision analysis, not a quantified dose assessment.

The minimum reader model: **shutdown stops the chain reaction; radioactive decay keeps making heat; that heat still needs a path out.** The concern is overheating, damaged fuel and possible radioactive release—not an instant atomic detonation. Fuel damage, failure of containment and offsite exposure are distinct outcomes. [1,2,9]

## 3. Design-specific dependency comparison

These are design families and open questions, **not six complete plant safety assessments**. NRC profiles directly checked representative units; Georgia Power confirms both Vogtle pairs. [3]

The claimed seven-day diesel supply remains unverified against readable applicable unit technical specifications in this pass. |
| Vogtle 3/4 | Westinghouse AP1000 PWRs; passive core/containment design and separately analyzed pool protection | A 72-hour design capability is neither measured water remaining nor a universal failure time. |
| Hatch 1/2 | Both NRC profiles specify GE Type 4 boiling-water reactors (BWRs), wet Mark I containment | Do not copy PWR cooling paths or AP1000 durations. Hatch-specific sustained cooling, DC, water and pool assessments remain incomplete. |
| Sequoyah | NRC Unit 1 profile: Westinghouse four-loop PWR, ice-condenser containment; TVA confirms both units are PWRs | Same broad vendor family as legacy Vogtle does not mean identical containment, backup or margins. |
| Watts Bar | NRC Unit 1 profile: Westinghouse four-loop PWR, ice-condenser containment | No unattended duration established; unit-specific analyses still needed, including Unit 2. |
| Oconee | NRC Unit 1 profile: Babcock & Wilcox lowered-loop PWR; **10 CFR 50.72 expressly identifies hydroelectric facilities used instead of emergency diesels at Oconee** | “Every plant runs until its diesel tank empties” is already wrong as a regional model. Hydro emergency power is not proof of indefinite unattended cooling. |

For conventional plants, track **offsite AC, onsite emergency AC, station DC, water inventories, heat rejection, and equipment state separately**. Station blackout means loss of offsite and onsite emergency AC, not simply cessation of generation; batteries/inverter supplies are distinguished in the regulatory definition. The blackout rule requires an analyzed *specified duration*, based on plant-related factors—not one nationwide number. [2]

Steam-driven does not mean independent of everything electrical or inexhaustible. The IAEA describes BWR reactor-core-isolation cooling (RCIC): reactor steam drives a pump, while water and a heat sink remain necessary. Its Fukushima analysis shows important DC-control dependencies and different real performance between units. **That is mechanism evidence, not a verified Hatch endurance figure or proof of Hatch's current equipment alignment.** Legacy-PWR auxiliary-feedwater actuation and steam/DC dependencies need applicable unit documents before detailed claims are placed on-page. [1,4]

Emergency diesel fuel stock is one prerequisite, not a reliability guarantee: availability, starting, supporting systems, load, faults and replenishment matter. Conversely, no source read establishes that a healthy diesel invariably requires a particular manual action by a particular hour. Do not invent that deadline. Post-Fukushima mitigation strategies explicitly include trained personnel and offsite assistance; portable backup equipment is not an autonomous extension simply because it exists onsite. [2,5,9]

## 4. What AP1000's 72 hours actually establishes

The regulator's 2011 certification explanation is more useful than the manufacturer's slogan. [6]

- **Core and containment:** Westinghouse describes 72 hours without operator action for core cooling and continued core/containment cooling afterward **with operator water-transfer actions**. NRC describes containment heat rejection through gravity-fed water on the steel shell and natural airflow. In its discussed accident/blackout conditions, continued wet cooling after the initial interval depends on ancillary equipment and replenishment. These are design conditions, not John's live margins. [6,7]
- **Air versus water:** natural airflow is a separate heat-transfer mechanism; it does not acquire a switch-off time simply because a quoted water interval ends. That does not establish that air alone meets every subsequent cooling need. Do not turn either “72 hours” or “passive” into an all-conditions guarantee.
- **Spent-fuel pool:** NRC's “Spent Fuel Pool” subsection says the first 72 hours after loss of normal cooling rely on water's heat capacity and boiling, including credited safety-related water sources. Protected sources support makeup beyond that interval, up to seven days in the described design. This is **not** a second reactor tank measured to be full, automatic unlimited pool cooling, or a prediction that fuel uncovers at hour 73. [6]
- **Later resources:** NRC describes ancillary generators/pumps and water/fuel replenishment for continuation. Westinghouse explicitly qualifies “indefinitely” with operator actions. No opened source establishes that all required post-72-hour functions happen automatically in an entirely unattended Vogtle unit. Equally, failure exactly at 72 hours is not established. [6,7]

Thus 05's compressed “72 hours … then water makeup” is directionally defensible as a **design reference**, but should not imply all three safety functions share one physical timer or a guaranteed cliff.

## 5. Core, pool, dry cask: three different questions

**Core:** recently operating fuel still makes decay heat after a successful trip. Serious core-cooling loss can matter before a hypothetical weeks-later pool problem. Fukushima Unit 1's damage occurred within hours under its particular flooded, power/control-loss conditions; other units progressed differently. This is contrary evidence to “the core is low-heat and safe after the diesels stop,” not an Atlanta forecast. [1, pp.56–58]

**Pool:** losing circulation is not the same as losing water cover. Water first warms; boiling can remove heat while consuming inventory; continued losses without makeup can reduce shielding and ultimately expose fuel. Starting temperature, available water, leakage, fuel loading and time since irradiation determine the trajectory. A recent full-core offload is materially different from mostly older fuel. A reactor trip does not automatically move its core into the pool. NRC's pool guidance emphasizes substantial inventory, structural robustness and recovery opportunities; its normal safety conclusion includes operator response. [1, pp.27,58;8]

**Dry cask:** sufficiently cooled fuel transferred from a pool uses a different storage system. NRC describes passive heat removal without fans or pumps. Loss of the grid does not cause dry casks to “boil dry.” Monitoring, aging and physical condition still matter; do not promise perpetual neglect-proof safety. No 2028 site-specific split between pool and dry storage was established. [10]

**Historical correction:** IAEA reports that Fukushima Units 3/4 pool levels did not reach the spent fuel; Unit 4's hydrogen came from Unit 3, not its own uncovered pool. Fukushima supports the decay-heat, power-loss and uncertainty lessons—not proof of a spent-pool fire or a transferable pool countdown. [1, p.58]

## 7. John's credible first-evening evidence inventory

**Preserve and label:** public reactor-design profiles; certification/safety documents and revisions; fuel-storage descriptions; latest pre-event/refueling records; dated public power reports; source-specific utility/grid observations if the novel actually establishes them; weather observations/forecasts and their last-update times. Keep collection time separate from measurement time.

**Do not silently supply:** actual diesel operability, usable fuel/water, battery charge, valve/system alignment, current pool heat load/level, completed operator actions, or a verified release source term. Public documents sometimes contain design inventories and historical conditions; that does not make their **current** values public telemetry. A refueling-outage label does not establish exactly where every assembly is now. A stale sensor value or failed communication path is not a measured plant failure.

NRC's public power report is labeled “unevaluated information provided by the facility,” collected in a daily morning window. Event reports originate in licensee notifications, not an always-on public automatic status feed. ERDS existence does not give John a public live dashboard or establish its activation/availability. Agency connectors do not automatically expose internal nuclear instrumentation. The 05 Georgia status table needs a named, bounded fictional evidence channel if retained. [4,12]

## 8. Hours, days, weeks—and a stay/leave rule

**Hours:** protective trips and backup responses can occur automatically, conditionally on design/state. Adequate cooling may persist; failed cooling can instead become serious early. No evidence establishes uniform success or failure.

**Days:** AP1000's sourced coping interval and conventional power/water/mitigation dependencies become relevant questions. Approaching a design interval increases the importance of unknown replenishment and actions; it does not prove release.

**Weeks:** declining decay heat helps, but continuing water loss or unrepaired failures may still matter. Neither universal pool uncovery nor universal safety can be assigned. Probabilities for permanent worldwide absence of personnel were not established by the sources read; normal accident-risk numbers cannot simply be reused.

**Editorial decision logic:** prepare mobility and preserve information now; compare remaining shelter/resources against transport/rescue opportunities; re-evaluate when credible plant degradation, a release, or deteriorating ability to leave changes the balance. Do not wait for a fabricated date, but do not manufacture a release as justification. A known release would require its timing, amount/material, wind and rain to assess route/destination—not “head west” as a universal rule. IAEA documents strongly weather-dependent deposition. [1,p.112]

No new distance was calculated or validated. The ten-site list remains a search inventory. Browns Ferry, V.C. Summer, Catawba, Farley and McGuire still need design/dependency review; all listed sites need unit-specific 2028 states. Palo Verde, Columbia, Hanford and naval reactors are outside this first pass. Port Angeles is not certified risk-free here.

## 9. Minimal scene translation and one author choice

1. Let John distinguish **stopped making electricity** from **still removing heat**, then ask about the pools separately. Existing 02/05 largely accomplish this without a lecture.
2. Let a single comparison teach the model: older Vogtle units, AP1000 units and Oconee's hydro-backed design do not belong on one timer. No equipment-operation description is needed.
3. Make his action concrete: preserve sources, prepare to move, keep searching for people. The cost of losing reliable information can create pressure without invented radiation readings or a pool-uncovery calendar.

**Highest-leverage later author choice:** is nuclear risk a precautionary reason to leave while travel/rescue remains feasible, or does departure require new observed deterioration? Recommend the former with present evidence. It preserves agency, uncertainty and the existing relocation/rescue plot without forcing an unsupported disaster.
