# Yacht / Dragon recovery: early feasibility and the local archive

**Baseline:** November 7, 2026; fictional mission in approximately June–July 2029. Read-only author research, not a lifting plan, hazardous-material procedure, navigation clearance or certification of a rescue. No repository changes, peer messages or automatic handoff. ISS food dates are expressly held; this note does not reopen them.

## 1. Verdict and the next decision

The chosen submersible-support expedition yacht is a **credible platform to investigate**, much stronger than the older reference's generic small explorer. Public evidence supports its working-deck heritage and a Dragon return mass of roughly the intended order. It does **not** establish that this particular yacht, lifting appliance and five-adult team can safely recover an occupied capsule.

The central gap is not simply “too heavy.” It is the complete chain: locating the vehicle, maintaining safe relative position, assessing hazards, connecting an approved interface, handling a moving load, placing it on a structurally supported cradle, and assisting four people while still operating both vessels.

**Highest-leverage author decision for later:** must the mission recover the **whole occupied capsule**, or is **people-first extraction** an acceptable conditional objective? NASA documents specialist contingency extraction without first hoisting Dragon, so the latter is not inherently impossible. It is not automatically easier or currently validated for this crew. Preserve the full-hoist canon until the author chooses; do not silently substitute a raft rescue.

## 4. What “the crane can lift ten tonnes” leaves out

### Which load, and which appliance?

NASA's Demo-2 mass is a useful **mission-specific comparison**, not a replacement for the fictional return manifest. Launch mass includes a trunk not recovered with the capsule. “Dry mass + maximum return cargo” is not a validated splashdown calculation: dry-mass definitions, crew, residual consumables/propellant and installed equipment must be reconciled. SpaceX's generic Dragon page quotes **3,000 kg return payload**, not total capsule mass or a guaranteed Crew Dragon loading option.[3,13]

The handling case must also distinguish capsule mass from the total suspended load, including lifting gear and any retained water/debris. No added-water quantity or arbitrary dynamic multiplier is assumed here. The applicable certified calculation must supply them where relevant.

The historical designer-hosted article is **February 2010 ShowBoats journalism**, not a manufacturer certificate. Its A-frame figures and later YachtBuyer text may reflect different units or descriptions. Fraser's actual broker listing also differs from YachtBuyer in draft, gross tonnage, crew accommodation and range: **4.95 m / 1,396 GT / 22 crew**, versus **4.68 m / 1,142 GT / 14 crew**; Fraser quotes shorter range figures.[1,2] These differences are not proof the fictional specification is wrong. They prevent treating mixed listings as one authoritative configuration. Gross tonnage measures enclosed volume, not a lifting-load mass.

### Geometry, motion and structure

A slewing crane needs capacity at the actual **radius, configuration and sea-state envelope**, not merely its maximum number. An A-frame has its own geometry/reeving/operating envelope; do not apply a pedestal-crane chart to it. The rig must reach the capsule, clear hull/deck edges and place it in the intended cradle. Its original submersible interfaces do not automatically fit a differently shaped vehicle and center of gravity.

A floating capsule and yacht move differently. As support transfers from buoyancy to the lifting appliance, forces and stability change. Side loading, abrupt tension and collision cannot be assessed from calm-water weight alone. BSEE Safety Alert 308 is an **offshore-platform casualty analogy**, not a Dragon simulation: it documents misuse of static instead of applicable dynamic limits and an incorrect chart.[4] It supports the need for the right engineering basis, not a numerical limit for Hydra.

The missing package is: appliance identification/load documentation; inspections and maintenance; approved capsule attachment/interface; qualified gear; vessel loading/stability and operating limits; deck/foundation reactions; cradle geometry, restraint and access clearances. “Della builds a cradle” can remain a strong character task, but it needs drawings and a reviewed load path, not only craftsmanship. Nominal total deck capacity does not resolve concentrated loads.

USCG's crane-record guidance identifies load tests, repairs, failures, replaced components and appliance records.[5] IMO's 2026 lifting-appliance changes likewise emphasize design, inspection, testing and maintenance.[14] Their legal application depends on vessel flag, status and appliance scope; **neither source certifies this fictional private yacht or establishes a universal legal manning/lifting rule for it**. Independent naval-architecture and lifting-engineering review remains necessary, especially with occupants inside.

## 5. Professional recovery versus this crew

NASA's **2025 Crew-10 and January 2026 Crew-11 Pacific accounts** confirm the basic normal sequence: two fast boats, spacecraft safety work, hoist with crew inside, then extraction and medical checks.[6] The older NASA overview explains capsule-integrity/hypergolic-vapor assessment and separate boat tasks; its Florida sites and detailed weather thresholds are historical, not a 2029 Pacific authorization.[7]

A functioning helipad is not a helicopter, qualified aircrew or guaranteed shore hospital. Catherine's planned care therefore needs a reachable, usable receiving place and a realistic interval aboard—not an unearned handoff to functioning institutions. Four returning astronauts may need simultaneous assistance. Plan for impaired mobility without declaring everyone necessarily unable to walk. The rescue does not end at opening a hatch.

### The people-first alternative—strictly conditional

NASA's contingency description includes trained rescue personnel entering the spacecraft after hazard assessment and moving occupants to a purpose-designed raft, without first deck-hoisting the whole vehicle.[7] This defeats the absolute claim that an afloat hatch opening inevitably sinks Dragon. It does **not** demonstrate that any hatch may be opened in any condition, or that untrained survivors can improvise the transfer.

People-first could remove the full-capsule lift/cradle requirement **only if** capsule condition, approved access/extraction provisions, trained personnel, suitable receiving craft and medical handling are established. It substitutes a difficult moving-platform transfer for a difficult lift. No hazardous-entry, propellant handling, rigging or evacuation instructions are supplied. Protective suits found in a warehouse do not by themselves create specialist capability.

### A workload test, not an invented staffing minimum

| Simultaneous function | Existing strength | Collision to expose in preparation |
|---|---|---|
| Yacht navigation/relative position | Johan; Sam assists | If Sam takes the tender, Johan still needs lookout/bridge support. Twin screws/bow thruster are not proof of dynamic-positioning capability. |
| Tender and capsule approach | Sam's seamanship | Who supplies the second trained set of hands, and how are they recovered? One boat is not automatically equivalent to NASA's two. |
| Crane/deck/engineering | Della's machinery skill | Machinery response, appliance control and deck supervision cannot all be assumed one simultaneous job. |
| Tracking/communications/lookout | John | Receiving updates, visual search and bridge duties may overlap. Cached forecasts cannot watch the capsule. |
| Extraction/medical | Catherine | Four patients plus transfers require helpers without abandoning vessel/boat control. |
| Support | Tyler | Noncritical logging or supplies may help; hazardous-task success cannot depend on treating him as an adult specialist. |

IMO's safe-manning principles emphasize competence, workload and fatigue rather than one magic crew count.[15] Three people listed as watchstanders does not itself prove adequate passage or rescue staffing. John is not established as a trained professional mariner. A rehearsal should expose tasks requiring the same adult at once; no arbitrary new rescue minimum is asserted.

## 6. Getting the yacht out and making her ready

USACE's operational large chamber is **760 × 80 ft**; the smaller chamber's usable **123 × 28 ft** cannot accommodate the approximately **183 × 39 ft** model.[8] NOAA's November 1, 2026 Coast Pilot gives the same main-lock dimensions and distinguishes actual surveys from project depths.[9] Thus “too big for Ballard Locks” is not the supported objection. The documented draft also exceeds the USACE threshold at which the saltwater-barrier arrangement becomes a passage consideration; no operating steps are reproduced.

Lake Union entails both locks and route-specific opening bridges. “Ballard yards” is insufficiently precise: the berth's side of the locks/bridges matters. The current Coast Pilot's bridge table gives clearances with central-span qualifications and water-level datums; without the yacht's **actual air draft and upperworks geometry**, no closed-bridge passage is certified. A mast cannot simply be assumed removable. Unanswered bridge/lock calls are an operator-dependency problem, not proof the structures have physically failed.

**Lowest-complexity candidate, not a retcon:** an author-selected saltwater berth could avoid these dependencies. Keeping Lake Union is possible as a premise but requires a credible, separately assessed passage solution; having a public boater leaflet is not operator training. No lock controls, bypasses or forced-entry methods were researched.

A months-idle vessel needs a condition-based survey/recommissioning and shakedown: propulsion, generators, steering, pumps/seawater systems, electrical supply, fire protection, communications/navigation, tenders and the lifting appliance. Do not universally declare batteries flat after a fixed interval. The yacht may have retained generation/charging; its actual unattended state is unset. Manuals and maintenance history matter more than a generic “turn the key” beat.

Brochure tank capacity is not fuel aboard; advertised range is not a passage budget including hotel loads, weather, maneuvering and reserve. No exact Seattle–Hook–California route distance, fuel quantity or voyage duration was calculated here. The existing ~60 nm/~75 nm Seattle figures need an exact departure berth and navigable track, not a straight-line comparison. Dependable Hook power remains settled canon, not new jeopardy.
