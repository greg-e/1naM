## 3. What a 911 record actually proves

NENA distinguishes call handling, CAD, incidents, and dispatch: an incident can involve multiple calls; CAD supports selected dispatch and recordkeeping functions. Its NG911 baseline describes interoperable routing, logging, and transfer of associated information—not a national database or identical local implementations. Historical NENA interoperability testing explicitly treats recorded media and call-processing event logs separately. [1–2]

For each lead, distinguish:

| Evidence | Supports | Does not establish by itself |
|---|---|---|
| Incoming call/event metadata | A system recorded a contact at a time | Human speech, successful triage, dispatch, or exact incident location |
| Audio | What the recorded caller/answerer said | Truth of every statement, current survival, or completed dispatch |
| Transcript or AI summary | A representation of recorded conversation | Perfect transcription, especially coordinates, names, negation, injury descriptions |
| CAD incident | A recorded incident/workflow entry | A unique caller or a unit actually responding |
| Dispatch/unit status history | Recorded assignments and acknowledgments | Physical arrival unless separately documented; human involvement if the fictional workflow is automated |

An AI saying “help is being dispatched” is not independent evidence that a dispatch event, acknowledgment, or response occurred. John's absent incident number also does not prove no call recording exists. Preserve the audio where available, link its event identifiers to any incident history, and label missing components separately. This is evidence interpretation, not an access procedure.

**Local boundaries matter.** Alpharetta's official 2026 dispatcher description lists jurisdiction checking, CAD, dispatch, and recording functions. Milton's official account identifies Alpharetta's center as covering Milton too. Consequently, a county label need not identify one PSAP or one backend. A “Fulton” result can remain fictional canon, but the author should know whether that means a geographic aggregation, a receiving center, or a particular archive. Actual routing/storage for John's calls is unverified. [3]

Georgia's published local-government schedule lists **three years** for 911 emergency call recordings, LG-19-001. It does not prove a particular call was recorded or that John can retrieve it. Alabama's 911 Board identifies the receiving local agency as the recording holder and distinguishes audio-release restrictions from other records. Huntsville's March 11, 2026 announcement describes approved capture/storage/retrieval of calls and planned deployment; approval is not proof installation was completed, much less proof of a 2028 record's availability. No nationwide retention period follows from these examples. [4–5]

**Location and callbacks are not universal certainties.** FCC rules distinguish dispatchable addresses, coordinate accuracy, uncertainty, and routing based on best available location when the preferred information is unavailable. Transfers can separate receiving jurisdiction from incident jurisdiction. Certain non-service-initialized 911 phones cannot receive callbacks. A carrier's retained location information is not the same record as PSAP audio. Do not describe every call as carrying an exact address and working callback. Della's spoken coordinates and bridge description provide a different, valuable location source; retain what she said separately from automated location fields. [6]

John's first recovered call therefore answers **“Can this route recover this known call?”** Ask about the second call too. Neither success nor failure establishes nationwide sensitivity. The 2028 AI may have answered many calls; “answered and logged every call” remains an unsupported universal assumption.

## 5. Aircraft and ships: observations, not occupant detectors

FAA describes ADS-B Out as automatic broadcasts of aircraft-derived position, altitude, groundspeed, and other data without pilot involvement. FAA guidance explains altitude/terrain line-of-sight limits; its surveillance publication explicitly maps estimated coverage at different altitudes. These sources do not validate global completeness, a particular commercial feed's latency, or John's fictional satellite/agency integration. [9]

For 00's nine lost tracks and 05's last ten, preserve event time, last reception, source, track quality, and the distinction between observed descent and inferred outcome. A descent away from an airport may be highly concerning; missing reception alone is not a crash certificate. Aircraft identity/flight plan is not an occupant manifest or proof of a conscious pilot. Unanswered calls to FAA/FEMA/USCG do not independently establish a global absence of human activity.

00's use of reaching **5,000 feet AGL** can help identify a tracked climb, but is not wheels-up time and excludes flights never reaching that threshold if used as an eligibility rule. Its separate departures/landings query needs its own historical coverage statement, not merely a count of aircraft still airborne. Retain the current bounded 05 table; revisit 06's “zero aircraft airborne” as an overconfident inference or a statement explicitly limited to queried feeds. Route schedules, aircraft performance, and the fictional counts were not verified here.

**AIS is especially valuable for Johan's delayed discovery.** IMO Resolution A.1106(29), paragraphs 10–14 and table 1, separates automatic sensor data from installed identity data and manually maintained voyage/status fields. Speed over ground, course over ground, and heading are distinct quantities; destination and ETA can remain unchanged without a person updating them. Paragraphs 32–39 warn that coverage and transmitted information may be incomplete or erroneous. Shore reception and John's archive are not the same as what a ship can receive locally. [10]

Thus 12's original course-change filter can plausibly miss Johan's straight-course slowdown. Changing the question later is a credible learning moment, not proof an AI should have found everyone immediately. However, **“An engine didn't fail in three-knot steps”** is stronger than these data establish: AIS reports motion, not engine commands. The slowdown is a candidate signature of purposeful activity, not a mechanically proven explanation. Excluding tide/current effects requires data and an uncertainty assessment; it cannot be assumed perfect. The retrospective one match among 106,160 evaluated vessels describes that archive, window, and signature—not the world's only occupied ship. The subsequent responsive voice exchange is much stronger human corroboration.

## 7. A bounded search and rescue standard

**Proposed author-facing workflow, not a software specification:**

1. Keep raw returned material and provenance: source/system, geographic and temporal scope, query window, access result, source timestamp/time zone, and retrieval time. Distinguish event time, later processing, and delayed arrival. Use UTC for comparison while retaining original zones; do not erase story-local times.
2. Separate **covered and empty**, **partially returned**, **inaccessible**, and **not searched**. A source becoming unreachable does not establish its retained records were destroyed.
3. Preserve links between calls, recordings, transcripts, CAD incidents, payments, and track reports; deduplicate repeated stages or upstream copies. Two sites repeating one feed are not independent corroboration.
4. Keep observations separate from model inference. Retain automated/uncertain records and filter reasons. An automatic crash/fall alert may indicate an incapacitated person; lack of intelligible speech is not proof nobody survived.
5. Use John's known cases as controls; test different times and routes. A successful positive control does not supply a numerical recall rate for other jurisdictions.
6. Record what would change each decision: narrower location, intelligible original audio, a fresh responsive exchange, or confirmation a supposed second source is merely a duplicate.

**Highest-leverage author choice: what makes a lead worth risking a rescue attempt?** Recommended working threshold: a credible post-event person-specific report, sufficiently actionable location, plausible continuing need, and a feasible attempt whose expected benefit justifies its risks. Seek independent corroboration when time allows, but do not require two proofs as a universal gate: Della's injury report and spoken location can justify action before renewed contact. This is a narrative decision rule, not validated search-and-rescue doctrine or certainty she remains alive.

Catherine's repeated calls and location support a different inference from Huntsville's repeated calls with unavailable conversation; repetition alone does not identify a competent helper or present medical status. Porto remains a real search lead without necessarily being a dispatchable rescue destination. Failed callbacks leave multiple explanations; 06's four-to-eight-hour tower-battery explanation is John's hypothesis, **not a verified diagnosis**. Telecom resilience and the actual failed-call path require separate research.
