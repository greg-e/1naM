## 4. Municipal water: storage can buy time, but which storage?

North Fulton is a **plausible geographic supplier**, not a house-specific determination. The county's 2026 Consumer Confidence Report identifies Chattahoochee source water, treatment at **Tom Lowe Atlanta-Fulton County Water Treatment Plant**, and subsequent elevated/ground treated-water storage. It lists nine elevated tanks, three ground tanks, five pump stations and two high-pressure zones.[2] No real personal address or private utility record was consulted.

The report also quotes two supply reservoirs totaling **895 million gallons**, described as 30 days of supply. **That is not 30 days of safe, pressurized water at John's tap without staff, treatment or pumping.** The same report separates source, treatment and finished-water storage. Its normal monitoring results mainly concern **2025**, not an unattended September 2028 system. No zone-specific emergency autonomy or backup-fuel inventory was obtained.

Elevated storage can sustain pressure through gravity after pumps stop; other supply paths require pumping. Duration depends on usable level, elevation/pressure zone, valves, leaks, continuing consumption, connected equipment and functioning backups. People's absence does not turn every fixture, irrigation controller or industrial demand instantly off. It also removes leak repairs and replenishment logistics. Pressure can therefore persist in one place and disappear elsewhere without contradiction.

EPA explains that lost pressure can admit contaminants through compromised distribution infrastructure.[1] Fulton’s **June 17, 2025 historical notice** describes a limited affected area, temporary restoration of service, and an advisory pending tests—not an automatic countywide contamination event.[2] This is a mechanism/example, **not a claim of any present advisory**.

Preserve 05's “possible supplier” caveat. John's useful actions are identifying public system information, recording whether service still flows, separating unknown quality from known failure, and inventorying an independent safe reserve. There is no basis here to assign his house a shutdown date.

The old grid reference's “at least the first day…roughly a week or two” is already labeled historical/deferred. Keep it retired as a prediction. Its blanket implication that ordinary household pressure is independent of live power, and that boiling always answers subsequent quality concerns, needs qualification—not a replacement universal timer.

## 6. Sanitation: the downstream half

Water entering a home and waste leaving it are different systems. Gravity fixtures can drain while a downstream lift station or treatment works loses function; conversely, having reserve drinking water does not establish usable sewer service. EPA identifies sewage backups and untreated environmental discharges as **possible**, not inevitable, power-loss consequences.[1]

Municipal water in the manuscript does not itself establish whether John's house is sewered or uses an onsite system. A successful flush would not prove the entire disposal path remains healthy. No household backup event is established; do not manufacture one.

For longer habitation, count drinking/food uses separately from washing and flushing, and establish a waste path appropriate to the actual system. Hand hygiene and separation of raw-food contact surfaces from ready-to-eat food protect the existing abundance. A doctor's presence or bottle of hand gel is not a substitute for sanitary water and waste handling. No improvised chemical toilet or sewage-handling procedure is prescribed.

Aboard, EPA distinguishes **holding** sewage from **treating** it: Type III storage does not itself treat the contents, while flow-through systems need their actual equipment and maintenance.[10] Receiving facilities, pumps, consumables and remaining capacity can matter. Discharge restrictions also differ by water/location and vessel category; the EPA overview distinguishes military graywater rules from recreational ones. Do not apply a yacht rule as a full legal/plumbing specification for a fictional cutter, or assume flushing means harmless disposal beside the drinking-water intake.

## 8. Refrigeration and the traveling cold chain

The familiar guidance holds under its assumptions: an unopened refrigerator stays cold for about **four hours** after cooling stops; a full freezer about **48 hours**, half-full about **24 hours**, doors closed.[7,8] These are not expiration guarantees for every package or clocks triggered by a regional blackout. 05 already gets that distinction right. John's brief transfer event does not imply hours without cooling; his deliberate generator shutdown next morning is a different event.

USDA/FDA recommend refrigerator temperatures **40°F or below** and freezer **0°F or below**. FoodSafety.gov's item-specific chart uses temperature/exposure categories—many common perishables exposed above 40°F for more than two hours are discarded—while FDA adds a shorter room-temperature allowance in heat above 90°F.[7,8] The closed-appliance heuristic is not permission to keep warm meat for four hours. Use the relevant item guidance rather than constructing one universal spoilage threshold.

Smell, taste and appearance cannot certify safety; cooking thoroughly does not reliably rescue time/temperature-abused perishables.[8] Conversely, “all we'll have are dry and canned goods” in 07:7 is an understandable fear, not a researched rule. Some intact produce and other foods need no refrigeration, and *Active*'s preserved cold stores are already on page. No new food shortage is needed.

The “ten below” reading in 09 supports working refrigeration; put units on the operational record before comparing it with guidance. One current reading does not reconstruct an unknown past excursion, but the established continuous shore-power premise and log are positive evidence—not grounds to invent thawing.

A DC-powered cooler may be an effective refrigerator or a different cooling technology; the unnamed model, control range and ambient performance are unknown. “Plugged in” does not establish that sandwiches/chicken remained cold through loading, road travel, stops and flight. A temperature record is the small missing evidence, not an elaborate new scene.

The actual **JE-5000A manual** confirms **5,040 Wh** nominal storage and **7,200 W** maximum AC output.[9] Watts describe rate/capability; watt-hours describe energy. Runtime requires usable state of charge, average combined load, cycling/starting behavior, conversion/idle losses, temperature and actual charging. Cooler, dev box and other loads share that budget. Output limits and energy-saving behavior also need a compatible-load check. No runtime or successful solar recharge is certified; no new equipment failure is inferred.
