## Objective 5: Assess infrastructure dependencies and preserve records

John needs priorities and alternatives, not a calculated hour when every service fails. The old exact-hours objective was a planning assumption, not a supported model output.

### Variable 1: Grid observations

- **Data:** EIA-930 hourly demand/history, separately from provisional frequency-monitor reports. EIA generation normally arrives the next day; its product includes neither frequency nor individual unit-trip states. No faster telemetry source or credentials are silently assumed. [EIA documentation](https://www.eia.gov/electricity/930-content/about.html)
- **Interpretation:** Automatic primary response, AGC, protection and voltage control can sustain or stabilize service, within resource and equipment limits. Demand changes despite human absence. There is no universal two-hour grace period, coal-fuel deadline or 59.5-Hz collapse cliff. Load shedding can preserve service; it is not the same as generator tripping. [NERC balancing reference](https://www.nerc.com/globalassets/who-we-are/standing-committees/rstc/rs/reference_document_nerc_balancing_and_frequency_control.pdf)
- **Output:** Latest provisional observations, last responses and reporting gaps. Generation percentages require the same reporting set at both times. The house's 00:50 September 6 utility loss is a local observation, not confirmation of a midnight Eastern Interconnection prediction.

### Variable 2: Network and service reachability

- **Data:** Available collector views, actual source responses and successful archive/copy records. A visible BGP route is not a powered-machine count or proof the requested service works.
- **Interpretation:** Power at John's house, network reachability and the destination service are distinct. Backup fuel figures or permits do not measure current operability or guarantee runtime. Satellite paths can vary; a working terminal does not put remote servers, gateways or their dependencies in orbit.
- **Output:** Last successful response, retained history, source coverage and archive status; prioritize accessible records that may be lost. No exact hour of losing every outside connection is established. [Starlink path/dependency description](https://starlink.com/public-files/StarlinkLatency.pdf)

### Variable 3: Municipal Utilities (Water & Gas)
The Architect must know how long their physical shelter remains habitable.
*   **The Data:** Water-utility and pipeline control systems (SCADA). **John has no authorization for these.** Reaching them would be a second unauthorized intrusion, and the safety layer comes off only once (Section 5). Use public utility data (tank capacities, pump-station locations), or leave this for Della to work out on the ground.
*   **The Model:** It calculates the remaining volume in local gravity-fed water towers and compares it against the drop in pressure from automated systems left running (like broken pipes or automated sprinklers). 
*   **The Output:** An estimate of when the taps in the Architect's building will run dry and when the natural gas lines will depressurize.

### Variable 4: Environmental Hazards (The Threat Radius)
An unattended world is highly volatile. The AI must ensure the Architect is not in a blast zone.
*   **The Data:** The AI scrapes EPA databases for nearby chemical refineries, fertilizer plants, and nuclear reactors.
*   **The Model:** Nuclear plants SCRAM safely within hours; the danger is the spent fuel pools once backup diesel and batteries run out, ~10–21 days after the trip (Section 5's *Sept 16*; see `power-grid-shutdown-timelines.md`). Chemical refineries require complex, human-supervised shutdown procedures to bleed off pressure. The AI runs atmospheric plume models based on current weather data.
*   **The Output:** A map overlay detailing toxic out-gassing threat zones and secondary fire risks from unmanaged industrial facilities in the Architect's immediate vicinity.
