# Johan Brandt

### 9/13/2028, 14:30 ship's time

Vancouver Island came up on the radar, a hard edge along the top of the screen at forty-eight miles. It was the first land he'd had since Japan.

His passage plan ended at a container berth in Seattle. Past Port Angeles a ship this size took a pilot for Admiralty Inlet and the Sound, and after that two tugs and a gang of linesmen to put her alongside, and he had no way to get a line ashore even if he got her there. He ruled the track as far as the harbor, inside Ediz Hook, and pricked off an anchorage in the middle, south of the pilot station, clear of the log booms and the box on the chart where anchoring was forbidden. The harbor was sixty miles past the entrance. He could do that in one day of light, and he wasn't going to try it in the dark.

At three he walked forward between the stacks with a hammer and a flashlight and went up on the forecastle. He knocked out the pin on the starboard chain stopper, cleared the lashings, and ran the windlass in and out of gear to see that it would answer. That left the stopper bar and the brake for the morning. He'd have to stop her on the engine from the bridge and then walk back up here to let go. He paced off the walk going back.

At six he called the traffic center at the entrance.

"Tofino Traffic, Tofino Traffic, this is motor vessel *Pacific Tender*, inbound Juan de Fuca, two zero miles west of Juliet buoy, over."

He called twice more, got no answer, and left it on 74, with 16 on the other set.

He took his phone out on the wing at dusk. On the last day of any crossing the crew came up here, the oilers and the cadets and the cook in his apron, holding their phones up along the rail as the coast came in range, and the messages landed all at once, two weeks of them. He held the phone up. It searched for a while and said *No Service*. His message to Lena was at the top of the screen.

The light on Tatoosh Island came up low ahead a little after eight. Vancouver Island showed no lights to the north, and neither did the coast to the south around Neah Bay.

He went in and rang her down to dead slow to wait for morning, and wrote it in the log: 2015, Cape Flattery light bearing 085, fourteen miles.
