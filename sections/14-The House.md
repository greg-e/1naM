# The House

### 9/16/2028, 11:40 local Pacific Time - John

"Active, Sam. We're stopped at a house with an antenna. John's got the address. I'll read it to you."

He did, and Johan read it back. John took two masks from the box behind the pickup's seat and put one on the dash beside Sam's hand.

"We may not get to the field," Sam said. "I'll call before we leave here."

Johan acknowledged. Sam clipped the radio back on and got out.

The gate dragged on the concrete path. John lifted it enough to get it past the corner and left it open. Sam knocked, waited, and knocked again. John called through the letter slot. The smell came out when he pushed it open, and he let it close.

The front door was locked. They went down the side of the house, past a hose wound on a reel and a blue recycling bin, and found the back door unlocked. John put his mask on before he tried it.

The kitchen had a plate in the sink and a loaf of bread in a plastic bag on the counter, green along one side. Sam called from the doorway, waited, then went in. John followed him through to the front room.

The man was in a recliner facing a television. His head had fallen toward his right shoulder, with his glasses crooked against his cheek, and his slippers were still on. John stopped at the kitchen door. Sam went past him and looked down the hall, then came back.

"One bedroom's an office. The other one's empty."

John went to the recliner. There was a remote under the man's hand and a photograph on the table beside him, the same man standing between two boys in baseball shirts. The smaller boy had both hands around a trophy. John looked for a name on the back of the frame, then put it down where it had been.

"There's a license in there," Sam said.

The office was the bedroom at the back. The door wouldn't open all the way because a folding chair was behind it, with a padded equipment bag on the seat. John squeezed past and stood clear for Sam.

A framed license hung above the desk. John read the name, *Arthur Keene*, and wrote it in his notebook. Through the window he could see the mast rising out of its brackets on the back wall. Two thick cables came through a plate below the sill, ran along the baseboard, and came up behind a desk crowded with equipment.

There was a radio, a power supply, a laptop with its lid down, and a microphone on a small stand. A pair of headphones was plugged into the front of the radio, and its face had more buttons than John could read from where he stood. On the shelf above it sat manuals, a soldering iron and a row of small plastic drawers with handwritten labels. A photograph of a black dog was pinned to the side of one drawer.

Sam moved the bag off the folding chair and put it on the floor. He unzipped it and lifted out a coil of cable, then held the bag open for John. There were loose adapters in one pocket and a small notebook in another.

"We take this with it," John said. "Even if we don't know what it's for."

He put his phone's light on and took a picture of the back of the radio before he touched a cable. The plugs were crowded together, with two leads disappearing behind the drawers. He moved the drawers far enough to see that one went to a speaker and the other to the laptop.

Sam held the light while he took another picture.

"Do you want to bring the desk?"

"Give me a minute."

"I'm asking."

John looked at the door they had barely got through. "No."

He found the power strip under the desk and unplugged it from the wall. A gray cable from the back of the radio was fastened to a copper bar behind the shelf. He photographed that, too, and followed it with the light until it went through the wall.

"We can't take all of this," he said.

Sam crouched beside him. "What stays?"

"Anything that goes outside. For now. I want to know what we're disconnecting first."

He got the tape and pencil out of the truck. Sam tore strips while John marked the plugs and the sockets they came out of, giving both ends the same number. The connectors on the power leads were different from the plugs at the rear. John checked each lead back to the supply before he removed it, and kept the supply beside the radio it had fed.

With the equipment pulled forward, he could reach the manuals. One was for the radio. He matched the picture on the cover to the front panel and put it on top of the radio. An antenna with a short handle and rows of metal elements leaned behind the door, its cable looped around the handle. John moved it out of the way with both hands.

A sheet taped to the wall had been hidden behind it. *ARISS* was printed across the top, above a table of frequencies and operating modes.

He read down to *Voice* and stopped with his finger against the paper.

"This is the station."

Sam came around the chair.

"Where?"

John pointed to *International Space Station*, then the two columns beneath it.

"Downlink's what we listen to. Uplink's what we send on. Different frequencies."

He took a picture and lifted the tape from the wall, holding the corner so it wouldn't tear. There were other frequencies below the voice entries, and notes in pencil in the margin. He put the whole sheet inside the radio's manual.

Sam held out an open logbook. "He's got it here, too."

John took it. Most of the pages were ruled columns with dates, times, callsigns and reports. Some entries had a town or country written at the end. He turned back through August, then July. Sam put his finger on an entry with *NA1SS* in the station column and *ISS* beside it.

There was another farther back, on a different date.

John looked at the license again, then at the chair. A foam cushion was tied to its back with two lengths of cord. On the desk, beneath where the log had been, was a grocery receipt with a list written on the reverse: milk, bread, dog food. The last one had been crossed out.

He closed the log and put it with the manuals in an empty printer-paper carton under the desk.

"Do you know how to set it up?" Sam asked.

"Not yet."

Sam took the radio out to the truck while John gathered the laptop, its charger and the leads that had been attached to it. The power supply was heavier than he expected. He carried it against his stomach with its cable looped over one wrist.

They wrapped the equipment in a blanket from behind the seat. The antenna wouldn't fit safely across the cab, so they put the bag and a folded part of the blanket under it in the pickup bed and secured it without bending the elements. The mast and the wires outside the house stayed where they were.

John went back in for the manuals. The office door swung farther now the chair had moved, and he could see the dust along the wall behind the desk. He checked under it once more for a loose lead, then went to the front room.

He had no bag for the man. The linen cupboard held a folded white sheet, and he brought it back and opened it over the chair, working it down over the slippers without moving him. He left the photograph uncovered on the table.

In his notebook, below the name, he wrote *front room, recliner*. He added the address and the date they had found him.

Sam waited at the back door with the carton. John checked that the rear latch would open from outside before he pulled it shut. At the truck Sam called the ship and told Johan they were coming back without going to the airport.

### 14:10 local Pacific Time

Johan met them at the pickup. He had changed into a Coast Guard sweatshirt with the sleeves rolled back, and he stood looking at the antenna while Sam undid the straps.

"This one goes inside first," John said, handing him the carton. "Books and the laptop."

They carried the equipment to the wardroom in three trips. John put the carton on the table, took the radio out of the blanket and laid the numbered leads beside it. He set it in front of his chair with its manual and put the folded frequency sheet beside them.

Johan picked up the manual and looked from the cover to the radio. He rested his hand on the top of the case.

"You know this one?"

"No. Ship radios. Not this."

He sat down and opened the manual. Sam put the power supply on the table and set the antenna on two empty chairs with its cable gathered underneath. He looked toward the windows.

"Those wires are still on the house."

"We need to work out what to put here," John said. "And where. I don't want to just connect it to whatever's on the roof."

He had labeled the cable that went to the copper bar but hadn't brought one to replace it. He wrote that down before he forgot. The mains lead had come out with the power strip; he put both beside the supply, away from the ship's outlet.

The laptop's battery was flat. John connected its charger to the strip and plugged that in by itself, then left it charging while he sorted the paper. He had the logbook open when Tyler came in.

The boy stopped by Sam, looking at the equipment on the chairs. Sam moved aside and let him past.

"Is that for her?"

"We're going to try it," John said.

Tyler touched the edge of the antenna's handle, then took his hand away. "Can I see?"

John slid the log around and put his finger on the entry Sam had found. Tyler leaned over it. He read the station column and then looked along the line to the date.

"That was before."

"Yes."

"Did he talk to them?"

"That's what he wrote."

"Where is he?"

John turned the book back toward himself. "He died. We found him at home."

Tyler ran his finger along the entry again. "What was his name?"

"Arthur Keene."

Tyler kept his finger on the line. John left the book where he could read it.

Johan looked up from the manual. He picked up the loose frequency sheet and held it beside the open page. John pulled his chair over so they could both see it.

"This is different from the channels we've been using," John said. He pointed to the voice entries. "We receive here, transmit here. We need to find how this set does that."

Johan followed the line across the sheet, then opened the manual to the contents. He ran a finger down the page and turned farther into it. Sam went to the galley and came back with three mugs, putting one at the unused place beside John.

Tyler had brought a chair over. He sat with his knees together and his hands between them, watching Johan turn the pages.

John put the log aside, with a strip of paper marking the station entry. He had been about to tell Tyler they could be a while, but the boy was looking at the manual now, trying to read it upside down. John turned it a little so he could see.

He wrote the two voice frequencies at the top of a clean notebook page. Beneath them he copied the manual's page number and put the notebook between himself and Johan.
