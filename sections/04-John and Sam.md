# John and Sam

### 13:45 - John

John pulled up to the gate he always used to drive to the back parking lot behind the Quality Aviation hangar that was close to where his 182 was kept.  The security pad worked and the automatic gate opened. There was only one other car parked in the lot, John remembered it had been there on Sunday as well.  He grabbed his flight bag and walked with Dan to his hangar.

The Cessna 182 was just as he had left it on Sunday.  Dan jumped up and sat in the right seat, John flipped on the master switch and brought the power up for the radios.  He put on his headset and plugged it in.

He dialed the Tower frequency and keyed the mic. "Peachtree Tower, anybody on this frequency?" Nothing. He dialed Approach. "Atlanta Approach, anybody?" Nothing. He dialed Center. "Atlanta Center, anybody?" Nothing. He went back to Approach, figured that was who a pilot coming into Atlanta would call.

Then he waited, Dan lying on the seat next to him. He opened FlightAware on his phone and pulled up DAL27PDK. It was past Chattanooga, coming down out of 20,000 feet. Maybe whoever was flying it wasn't listening to anything. Maybe it wasn't coming here at all, and PDK on the flight ID meant something else. Maybe whatever had killed everyone else had just taken longer with whoever that was, and John was about to watch it fly into the ground.

There was one frequency every airplane listened to.

### 14:08 - John

John dialed in 121.5, the emergency frequency and got his thumb on the transmit button.

Before he could press it, his headphones came alive, and John's stomach jumped as a voice came through the radio.

### Sam

The last leg ran southeast over the farmland of Iowa and across the Mississippi, over Illinois, Kentucky, Tennessee. He kept the transmissions going on the hour and the half hour. He was finding it hard to keep his mind on flying. Every mile closer to Atlanta, his stomach knotted tighter.

Over the Cumberland Plateau the haze came up to meet him, a white layer with the ridges of the Appalachians standing up out of it to the east. He started down at Chattanooga. Atlanta rose out of the haze ahead, the towers downtown and Midtown, then Buckhead closer, and north of them the trees of Dunwoody, his street under them somewhere, where Elena's car would be in the drive and Rosa's bike against the garage.

Twenty miles northwest of the field, Sam had Atlanta Approach on one radio and 121.5 on the other, and on the descent he keyed 121.5 again, the same call he'd made on the hour and the half hour since Edmonton.

"Any station, Pilatus Charlie Golf Kilo Papa X-ray, transmitting in the blind. Twenty miles northwest of Atlanta, descending through eight thousand, landing DeKalb-Peachtree."

Then a voice in his headset, on 121.5. A man, unsteady. "Kilo Papa X-ray, this is — this is Cessna Four Six Five Hotel, on the ground at PDK. I can hear you."

For a second Sam's voice wouldn't work at all. "Say again," he finally got out, rougher than he'd sounded once in ten hours of talking to dead air. "Say again. Who is this?"

Nothing came back for several seconds. Then John's voice again, steadier. "John Hartman. I live up in Alpharetta. I saw DAL27 had landed in Edmonton, and I put together you were headed to PDK. I'm on the ground calling from my 182."

"John, you're the first voice I've heard since last night. Is there anybody else there with you? Anybody at all?"

John took a moment to answer. "There's nobody," he said. "It's me. Just me and my dog."

The frequency went quiet. Sam heard his own breathing in the headset. "Copy," he said, flatter than he meant it. "Ten minutes out. See you on the ground."

Out of habit he called Peachtree Tower on its published frequency: "Peachtree Tower, Pilatus Kilo Papa X-ray, ten miles northwest, landing PDK." Nothing. There was nobody in the tower to give him the wind. He went back to 121.5.

"John, Papa X-ray. What are the winds doing down there?"

A few seconds, then John's voice. "Winds are favoring two-one."

"Copy. Thanks."

### John and Sam

John shut down the radios and turned off the master switch on the 182.  "Come on Dan, let's go."  They headed back to the car and pulled it around to the Epps hangar.

Sam put the gear down and felt it lock, ran the flaps out to full, and let the speed come back to eighty-five knots. The four lights beside the runway showed two white and two red: right on the glide path. At a thousand feet he clicked off the autopilot, and the airplane was his. He searched the runway ahead to make sure it was clear. He held it off and let the mains settle on, lowered the nose, pulled the power lever back into reverse, and the propeller roared and the airplane slowed to a walk in a fraction of the runway. He turned off at the next taxiway and followed it to the ramp in front of the hangar at Epps.

John got out of the car with Dan at his side. He saw the PC-12 come out of the haze low and steady, gear down, flaps out. He watched it touch down on 21L, heard the propeller roar in reverse and fade across the field.

The airplane taxied up and stopped on the ramp in front of the hangar, the propeller winding down to a stop. John stood there, looking at the windshield of a white-and-gray PC-12 with C-GKPX in black letters on its side, he was no longer alone.

The door behind the wing unlatched and swung down, the steps unfolding. A man came down them. He wore a Delta officer's dark jacket, three gold stripes on each sleeve catching the afternoon sun, cap in one hand. John imagined he looked the way he would have standing at the flight deck door seeing people off at the gate. At the bottom of the steps he stopped.

There was John, thirty yards off next to the FBO, a dog already running toward him: a tall, rangy man in a ball cap and black-framed glasses, a thick brown beard shot through with white, olive drab cargo pants and flip-flops.

"Should I be concerned?" Sam called out, eyes on the dog.

"Not at all," John called back, heading toward the plane. "He's excited to see you. His name's Dan."

Dan got there first, pressing himself against Sam's legs same as he did with anyone in the family. Sam went down on one knee, patting the dog's side and scruffing his head and ears. John got there, flip-flops slapping the concrete, and Sam got back up to his feet, straight-backed even now. John stood there a second, looking at the face of another living person: clean-shaven, short black hair gone salt-and-pepper at the temples, olive skin, brown eyes rimmed red.

John held out his hand. "John."

Sam took it and laughed, a short, broken sound. "Sam. Samuel Reyes." He held on a second longer than a handshake needed. 

For the next ten minutes they stood on the ramp beside the PC-12. Sam gave a summary of his morning: Jack, Theresa, Ruth, the cabin, the turn south over Saskatchewan and the landing at Edmonton, and the trip to Atlanta. John described his day so far: the thud at 2:05, the man in the ditch, Harry, the dogs, the frozen feeds, the empty sky, the drive to the field. 

"I need to get to my family in Dunwoody," Sam said. "Ten minutes from here. I need — " His voice caught and he pushed through it. "I need to go."

"I'll drive," John said. "Let's go."

### 15:05

Sam grabbed his flight bag from the plane, lifted the stairs and closed the door.  They took John's car north. Sam sat rigid in the passenger seat, taking in this strange new world: cars stopped at odd angles, a strip mall with its lot empty. Twice he had John pull over, once at a sedan folded around a light pole, once at a truck nosed into a ditch with its trailer jackknifed across the lane, Sam got out without a word of explanation, and looked at the people in the vehicles. He came back to the car each time quiet. Twice Sam tried Elena's number and only got voicemail. Quiet most of the way except for some directions from Sam.

### 15:25

They got to Sam's house, a minivan in the driveway, a kid's bike against the garage door. Sam was out of the car before John had it fully stopped.

Sam went in through the front door at a near run, calling their names, and John came up the walk slower, leaving Dan in the car, and stopped on the front porch. He stood in the open doorway.

It didn't take long. From a bedroom down the hall came a sound John had never heard a grown man make, lower and more ruined than crying. John left Sam alone and went back to the car. He got Dan out and walked with him a few blocks. Dogs barked in a few of the houses they passed. 

Sam stood looking at Elena lying in bed peacefully, the blanket up at her shoulder.  Sam sat down next to his wife and wept, a low moan coming from his chest, he stayed there for a while, hardly able to breath. Sam got slowly to his feet and went to Rosa's room. He slid his arms under her, and she came up off the mattress all at once, still curled on her side. Thirteen hours had set her in the shape she'd fallen asleep in. Sam held her against his chest like that, the way he must have carried her to bed a thousand times when she was small. Sam turned sideways so her feet wouldn't touch the frame and laid her on the bed beside her mother, on her side, facing Elena, close enough that her forehead nearly touched Elena's shoulder. He pulled the blanket up over both of them and stood there.

John came back half an hour later and sat down on Sam's front steps, and Dan lay down next to him.

When Sam came outside he'd taken the jacket off, and his face was pale, eyes red.

"I am so sorry, Sam." John paused. 

Sam looked at him a long moment. "With everything I saw today, I already knew there was very little chance they made it."

"Come back to my place and get some rest." John said.

"Yeah. Okay. I'll grab some of my things." and with that Sam went back into the house.

John went back to the car.

Sam came back a few minutes later, wearing a t-shirt and chino's and a backpack. He locked the front door behind him.

### 16:30

They pulled out of the driveway, Sam quiet in the passenger seat, Dan's chin resting on the console between them. The drive up to Alpharetta took an hour and a half, through the stopped, silent world, and neither of them said much until John pulled into his own driveway just before five thirty and into the garage, and cut the engine. They sat there a minute before either of them moved.

John broke the silence. "Come on. I'm hungry, I'll make something to eat."

### 17:30

Sam stood a moment in the front hall, looking at the photos on the wall.

John took off his hat, hung it on a hook next to the garage door, and went to the kitchen. Cooking settled him, and he had some chicken breasts he could grill. Ten years ago John had redone his kitchen to have a large flattop and gas burners just like Waffle house where he had worked nights for four years while attending Georgia Tech. "How do you like your hashbrowns?" John called out to the living room where Sam had found the couch. "Scattered, smothered, covered, and diced." Sam called back.

While the griddle was warming up, John went down the hall to the guest room. It had never once had a guest in it. Naomi had furnished it the year they moved in, for the company she'd expected to have, and it had sat that way for sixteen years with the door shut and the blinds down. He got sheets out of the hall closet and made the bed. In the guest bathroom he hung two towels that still had the store fold in them and ran the shower until the water came out of the pipes clear and then hot. He found a new toothbrush in the pack under his own sink and set it on the counter, and a bar of soap still in its paper.

John came back and grilled the chicken and made hashbrowns, he liked them the same way Sam did. They ate at the counter sitting on the bar stools, mostly quiet, Dan working a bowl of his own near the door to the garage. Sam ate everything on his plate and didn't seem to notice what it was.

### 19:10

"Take the first room down the hall on the left," John said when they were done. "Bathroom's across from it. There are towels."

Sam nodded. He sat a minute longer. "I'm going to take a shower and go to bed."

"Let me know if you need anything else, help yourself to anything. I am typically up by 6:30, espresso machine is over here and a kuereg." John pointed out where the coffee stuff was.

"Thank you, John." Sam got down from the stool and reached out and shook John's hand. "See you tomorrow."

Sam took his backpack down the hall. John rinsed the plates and listened to the shower run in the guest bath. It ran a long time. When it shut off he heard the bathroom door, then the guest room door, and then nothing. 

John went to his office, he had work to do.
