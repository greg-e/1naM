# Return to Ediz Hook

**12:00 local Pacific Time, Monday**

"Ninety-nine one," John said, and wrote it on the sheet with the time beside it. That made eight lines since Sunday morning, in the columns Catherine had ruled for him. Della took the plastic cylinder off the blanket and did ten on it while he counted, with Tran's meter by her good hand and the marine handheld hissing on 16. She got the piston a little higher than she had on Saturday.

He had worked it out in the notebook that morning. If they left Huntsville at first light, with an hour on the ground at Omaha and a headwind the whole way, they couldn't be here before one o'clock. The aviation handheld wasn't going to hear an airplane from inside all that steel, so from one on he took it and Dan up to the flight deck every half hour. The wind was out of the west at about ten knots, with high thin cloud and the Olympics clear behind the town.

**14:05 local Pacific Time**

The radio scratched and said something with Romeo in it.

"Two Papa Romeo, John. Go ahead."

It took two more tries before he could make out all of it. "...forty miles southeast, nine thousand, coming down. Three on board. Catherine's fine."

John stood there with his thumb on the button. "Say again number on board."

"Three. We've got a boy with us. He's ten. I'll be straight in on 26 in about fifteen minutes, and I'd appreciate it if you'd get the gulls off the runway."

"Wind's west at ten. I'll run the truck down it."

He went by the sick bay on his way off the ship. "They've got a boy," he said. "Ten years old."

Della looked at him with her head tipped back. "Go get your gulls," she said.

He took the white pickup down the runway at forty with Dan standing on the seat and the gulls going up ahead of him in sheets, then turned around at the driftwood and drove back. He had just parked by the hangar when he heard the airplane. It came around the end of the spit with the gear down, passed *Active*, and settled on the numbers, and the engine note went up as Sam brought in the reverse. It taxied onto the ramp and turned and the prop wound down.

The airstair came down and Catherine came first, with one hand on the rail and that care through her lower back. "Numbers," she said. John gave her the clipboard, and she started reading it where she stood.

The boy stopped at the top of the stairs. He was small and thin in the neck and arms, in a NASA shirt that had gone pale from washing, with a school backpack hanging off one shoulder and a heavy old Bible with a rubber band around it under his arm. He looked at the hangar and the ship and the water on both sides. Then he looked at Dan, who had hit the end of the leash and was up on his hind legs, whining.

"Is that Dan?"

"That's Dan," John said. "He won't hurt you. He might knock you down."

The boy came down the steps and sat on the bottom one, and John let the leash go. Dan got his front feet on the boy's knees and went to work on his face. The boy didn't laugh. He shut his eyes and got his free arm around the dog's neck and held on.

Sam came down with the chocks. His eyes were red, and he worked the fingers of his right hand before he put it out. "Tyler Vance," he said, with his voice down. "Huntsville. His uncle was on the couch at the bottom of the stairs, and the boy stayed up in his room with the door shut for six days."

John looked at the bottom step. "Browns Ferry?"

"I went by two miles north of it. Fan towers not turning, nothing off the stack. There's a fire at Decatur, and the smoke was lying right out to the east, over Huntsville."

"How much did you land with?"

"Five hundred pounds. It was a northwest wind all the way from Omaha."

They got the tie-downs on. The wind eddied around the end of the hangar, and it brought what was behind those doors out across the ramp. The boy lifted his head. He looked at the shut doors, then at Sam. "Are there..." he said, and he stopped.

"Seventeen," John said. "They worked here. We haven't gotten to them yet. There's none on the ship. I mopped it myself."

Catherine looked up from the clipboard at John. The boy thought about it and nodded. "Okay," he said, and he got up and picked up the leash.

At the top of the brow he stopped in the passageway and breathed in, fuel oil and floor wax, and let it out. John drew him a cup of water at the fountain outside the galley. He held it without drinking and looked along the row of doors. "How much is there?"

"How much water?" John had read the board in the control room. "Thirty-one thousand gallons in the tanks, as of this morning. There's a plant in the engine room that makes more out of the harbor, a few thousand gallons a day if you run it."

The boy set the cup on the deck, took a spiral notebook out of his backpack, and got a pencil out of the wire. "How many liters is a gallon?"

"Three point seven nine."

He worked it on the inside of the back cover, with his tongue in the corner of his mouth. He looked at the answer. "For five people."

"Tyler," Catherine said. "You can have all you want."

"I know." He wrote the number at the top of a clean page and put the notebook away, and then he picked up the cup and drank it.

Della had gotten someone's comb through her hair with one hand. Her eyes had gone from purple to yellow and green. John brought the boy in, and he stood at the foot of the bunk and looked at the splint and the sling and the plastic cylinder.

"Della, this is Tyler."

"Hey, Tyler," Della said.

"Does that..." He was looking at the splint.

"Yep." She looked him over. "You eat anything today?"

"Crackers. I threw up on the plane."

"Catherine. He's gray."

Catherine put the clipboard down and got her fingers on his wrist. Ten minutes later Sam took him along the officers' passageway. There was a two-man room next door to the one Sam had picked, and Sam looked at it, went and got his bag, and moved it in. The boy lay down on the lower bunk with his shoes on and the Bible beside him. Dan stood in the door and looked at John, then went in and lay down on the deck by the bunk.

**16:40 local Pacific Time**

John had the griddle hot and a pan of chicken thawing when Sam came into the galley with two grocery sacks. He put them on the steel counter, and they clinked. It was quart jars of peaches, with the year written on the lids.

"His grandmother's," Sam said. "She was across town, by A&M. I went in and found her. He wants them for supper."

John put the potatoes down thin and left them alone. Sam stayed. He poured a coffee and didn't drink it.

"He had his water rationed," Sam said. "Half a liter a day, and he was keeping a log of it. He had a little over a liter left when we got there. He'd have run out Wednesday."

John turned the potatoes and put the chicken on.

**18:00 local Pacific Time**

They ate in the sick bay, all five of them, with Dan under the exam table. Tyler sat on the stool with some color in his face and a plate Catherine had cut down by half. John had asked him how he liked his hash browns, and he'd said he didn't know. He ate them careful and then not careful, until Catherine put a finger on his plate. John opened a jar of the peaches and passed it around with a spoon, and Tyler watched each of them take one.

"Tyler," Sam said. "Show him."

Tyler got out the notebook and the picture. He looked at Sam first, and then he came over and held them out to John, the notebook opened to a column in careful pencil, with a lot of the lines checked off. Each one had a time, a direction, and a number. The picture was a small woman with short twists in a blue flight suit, with his grin.

"That's my mom," he said. "Kendra Vance. She's on the space station, since March. It still goes over. There's seven of them, four on the Dragon and three on the Soyuz. I saw it Thursday and I saw it Friday."

John looked at the picture. He had counted the aircraft and the ships and every reactor in two hundred miles, and he had a tower calling to the Strait every hour on the hour. He took out the pocket notebook and turned back to the page with the six questions. Next to *3. Is there anyone else alive?* he had *Samuel Reyes* and *Porto* and *SafetyNET 2141*. He wrote *ISS, 7?* He didn't look up right away.

"I never looked," he said.

"Me neither," Sam said.

"Are they..." Tyler said.

"I don't know, Tyler. I've got nothing on it. All I have from NASA is a file of observatory alerts, and nothing from anywhere newer than Wednesday morning." John gave the picture back. He looked at the notebook again, and then he looked closer. "What are these numbers?"

"That's how high it gets. In degrees. That's where it comes up, and that's when." Tyler put his finger on the column. "But they're wrong here. The app does it by where you are, and these are for my house. And they stop on Thursday."

"You copied the direction and the elevation for every pass?"

"Yes sir."

"How many have you got?"

Tyler counted. "Fourteen."

Della had stopped chewing and was watching John. He'd started to smile. "Bring that with you," he said.

John pulled up a second chair at the corner of the wardroom table, in front of the big screen, and Tyler read the lines off one at a time, a little hoarse, while John typed them in with the Huntsville address on top. Dan came and lay on his feet. Sam stood in the door with his coffee. John told the model to fit an orbit to the fourteen lines and give him the passes for the Hook. It took under a minute.

**14 predicted passes, 1 site. Assumes no reboost after source date.**

**Inclination 51.6°. Altitude 415 km. Period 92.7 min. Residual 8 sec. Error grows with time; rate unknown.**

**48.14 N, 123.41 W, next 7 days: 42 passes above 10°.**

**Station sunlit, sun below horizon: Mon 9/11, 19:54 PDT, WSW to SSW, max 11°. Next: none through 9/23.**

"What about the other forty-one?" Tyler asked.

"It goes over with the sun up. It's there, you just can't see it." John looked at the clock on the bulkhead. It said 19:21. "That one's tonight."

Tyler had turned around in the chair and put his arm out straight with his fist closed, sighting along it at the wardroom wall. "A fist's ten degrees. So that's only one fist and a little." He looked back at John. "What's over there?"

Mount Angeles was a little over a mile high and about ten miles south of them, and that took up most of the fist. He brought up the sectional on the screen for Sam.

"It'll clear by about one finger," Sam said. "Get a jacket."

**19:45 local Pacific Time**

The flight deck faced aft, down the length of the spit, and they walked over to the starboard side, where they could see across the harbor. The sun had gone down at the far end of the Strait. The town was dark along the foot of the mountains, and the sky over the ridge had gone pale yellow going to green. It was cold with the wind. Sam had found a Coast Guard foul-weather jacket that came down to Tyler's knees, and Tyler stood in it with the notebook against his chest and Dan leaning on his leg.

Sam had brought his headlamp and left it off. John had his watch. At 19:50 Tyler put his fist out over the ridge and walked it along to the right until he had the place, and he stayed like that.

"It might be too light," Tyler said.

"It might," Sam said.

"Now," Tyler said. John's watch said 19:54. Tyler's arm moved to the left, with his head going with it, and nothing was out there but the sky. John looked until his eyes watered. A gull went by low over the water, and he looked at that.

Sam had stepped up behind the boy. He stood with his head still and his eyes moving across one piece of sky and then the next, the way he had been finding other airplanes since he was a teenager. He lifted one flat hand. "Lower," he said. "Come down from your fist. Right of the notch, a finger over the ridge, moving right to left. Don't look at it straight. Look just to the side."

Tyler's arm came down and stopped. He didn't say anything.

John got it a few seconds later, a white point that didn't blink, sliding along above the black line of the mountains with the yellow sky behind it. It was brighter than he would have thought. He watched it pass the notch. Tyler's arm went along with it, and Sam had put a hand on the back of the big jacket. It went on to the left for about another minute, dimmer and lower, until it went behind the ridge.

Tyler kept his arm out a little longer. Then he brought it down and held out his hand, and Sam gave him the headlamp. He opened the notebook against his chest to a clean line, wrote *Mon 7:54, Ediz Hook*, and next to it he wrote *saw it*.
