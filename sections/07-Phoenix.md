# Phoenix

The day was clear, no clouds and temps in the mid 70s. They climbed to 28,000 feet, Sam continued the routine of the day before of trying to contact every station along the route. John climbed into the back and pulled out sandwich makings from the cooler. He got a couple bottled waters and the sandwiches and returned to the right seat in the cockpit.

"I could get used to your cooking." Sam smiled as he took a bite of the sandwich. 

"I've been thinking about food, with the power grid failure, we might be down to dry and canned goods if we can't keep things cold. I've never slaughtered a cow or even a chicken for that matter, you?" 

"Nope," Sam replied, "I've always enjoyed learning new things and right now I welcome anything that will keep my mind off whatever this is."

**13:50 EDT**

"Stopping for fuel in Amarillo, we'll start our descent in 8 min," Sam said.

Before landing at Amarillo Sam flew a low circle around the airport. The main runway, runway 4/22, what a disaster, what was left of a large aircraft was scattered over hundreds of yards. Fires burned in the fields next to the airstrip.

"Runway 31 it is," said Sam. "Looks like general aviation ramp over there anyway."

John called out the checklist and they flew a regular downwind pattern for runway 31, Sam pulled up the tower frequency and announced each leg.

"Amarillo, Pilatus Six One Two Papa Romeo short final 31."

**13:15 CDT**

They touched down and Sam kept the power up rolling all the way to the end of the runway. The FBO was adjacent to runway 22.

John pointed to a fuel truck with 'JETA' stenciled on the side. They parked on the ramp near it and spooled down the turbine.

"I need a pit stop," John said. "Me too and right behind you," said Sam.

Haven FBO was very nice, typical high end corporate place catering to jet-set CEOs. The doors were open and mercifully no one had been hanging out. No dead bodies to walk around or try to ignore.  

They found the keys to the fuel truck and working together got 2PR fueled.

**13:50 CDT**

No wind, they departed on runway 13 and climbed out south turning west toward Phoenix.

"Alright," Sam said, "Catherine said she'd stay at her apartment, but that was yesterday, before the power went. A hospital has generators, she may have gone back. I was thinking of flying low over every address she called from, the apartment, the fire station and the hospital, and then making several circles around the airport, see if we can get her attention." John nodded. "How are we doing on time?"

Sam looked at the iPad. "Around 15:00 local time in Phoenix, if we're gonna pull it off we will need to get Catherine within 40 minutes and top off again, we may not make it to Port Angeles today, hopefully Port Angeles can hang in there."

John thought for a minute. "Catherine seems like all business, should be easy to get her on board, anyway, it must be miserable there without AC, I hope she didn't head out."

**14:50 MST**

They came in from the east with John reading off the addresses from her calls. Sam descended to 800 feet AGL over Tempe and flew up South Rural Road over an apartment complex with the power lever all the way forward, then on across the Salt River to the fire station. Maricopa County hospital was just north of Phoenix Sky Harbor International Airport. He flew just to the south of the tallest building, pulled up and made a slow turn to the south and flew south to north over the hospital, then turned and made a wide arc around the airport, low with the power up, making as much noise as possible.

"That should do it, if she's at any of those she had to hear and see us, I'll make two more circuits and land on runway 25L over by that General Aviation FBO."

**15:15 MST**

They rolled out keeping power to the end of the runway where Cutter Aviation was.

2PR spooled down near a line of fuel trucks, next to a Gulfstream IV. Dan was anxious to get out. The heat felt like a brick wall when John opened the door. They stepped out into a beautiful albeit blistering Arizona afternoon.

Sam picked the Jet-A truck with the most on its gauge, found the keys on the visor and backed it up to the wing, the yellow beacon on the cab turning. John clipped the bonding cable to the airframe and dragged the hose over. The wing was too hot to keep a hand on.

John pulled up the address she'd left on her last call, South Rural Road in Tempe, number 214. The offline map put it about six miles away. "I'll see if they've got a crew car."

"Give me a hand on the left wing first, I need you on the meter," Sam said. "I want to be rolling by four-thirty."

**15:30 MST**

They finished the left wing and Sam moved the ladder to the right. Cutter's ramp door was unlocked. John took Dan in with him and a few steps inside he caught the smell. The lobby was dark and hot, the air conditioning gone with everything else, and a young man in a company polo sat at the front desk with his head down on his arms. Dan stopped and whined and wouldn't go any closer. John dropped the leash, told him to stay, and went behind the desk, breathing through his mouth.

The key board was on the wall behind the kid. John found a ring tagged CREW CAR and had it off the hook when three hard raps came from the front of the lobby, metal on glass.

John jumped and the keys hit the floor. His hip caught the kid's chair and it rolled a few inches. Dan was already barking, full voice, the hair up along his back, and he took off across the lobby with his leash dragging behind him. John stood with his back against the key board and his heart pounding and it took him a second to make sense of what he was looking at.

A woman stood at the front doors with one hand up to shade her eyes and a car key in the other. Dan was up on his hind legs against the glass, barking in her face, and she didn't step back.
He picked the keys up off the floor and went over. "Dan, sit. Quiet." Dan did neither.

The doors were automatic and dead. He got his fingers in the gap and walked one panel back a couple of feet, and Dan shoved through it first. As soon as he got his nose on her the barking stopped. She lowered herself to him with her back straight, the way you'd go down to pick up something heavy, and put both hands into his coat. She was small, with glasses and light brown hair, and she was wet, her hair dripping and her jeans dark to the hips. From the calls he had pictured someone older and taller. She didn't look much past thirty.

"Hi," she said. "Hi, you. Look at you." She turned his tag over. "Dan. Hello, Dan."

She got up the same careful way and took John's wrist before she said a word to him, two fingers, looking at his fingernails while she counted. "Any trouble breathing? Headache, nausea, anything?"

"No, I'm fine."

"You flew right over my apartment." She let go of his wrist and left her hand on his arm.

"Dr. Dahl?" John asked.

The hand came off. "How do you know my name?"

"You called 911 yesterday."

She stared at him.

"Long story, we're in a hurry."

"We?" She looked past him, through the lobby and the ramp windows to where Sam stood on the stepladder with the nozzle. "A unit will be dispatched," she said. "As priority allows."

"We came as fast as we could."

She started laughing and it turned into crying. She took off her glasses and wiped them on her shirt, which was still damp and only made them worse, and she kept wiping. John looked at Dan.

"I felt so stupid leaving that message," she said. "I didn't think anyone would ever get it."

"I'm glad you did, it's great to meet you Dr. Dahl, my name is John."

She smiled, "Please, call me Catherine."

She put her glasses back on and they walked through the lobby and out to the ramp.

Sam came down the ladder when he saw them and wiped his hand on his chinos. "Sam Reyes."

"Catherine." She took his wrist instead of his hand, and he let her, looking over at John with his eyebrows up. "Any trouble breathing?"

"No."

She let go of him. "You said you were in a hurry?"

"Show her," Sam said.

"There's a woman in Washington State," John said. "She called once." He brought up the transcript and handed her the phone.

She read it standing in the sun, moving it up with her thumb. "Left foot turned around backward, can't use the arm, and she's going to drag herself down a bank." She looked at her watch. "Monday, 23:19. Is that their time?"

"Yes."

"Forty hours."

"How far?"

"Four and a half hours," Sam said.

She handed the phone back. "Well let's go, my bags are in the car."

She started back toward the lobby with Dan beside her, his leash dragging.

John and Sam looked at eachother, "You were right, John, she is no nonsense."

John went after her with the crew car keys still in his hand. He reached past the young man and hung them on their hook, then went out front to help with the bags: a duffel, a case of water, and a hard-sided medical bag with her name stenciled on the lid. She carried that one herself.

Sam was coming down the ladder from the right wing when they carried the bags around to the airplane. "What do you weigh?" he asked Catherine.

"One twenty-five."

He hefted each of her bags, got the iPad out of the cockpit and added her to the weight and balance from PDK. It came out inside the envelope.

**16:10 MST**

Catherine took the forward cabin seat and Dan lay down on the floor by her feet. John handed her a headset and showed her where to plug it in. It was well over a hundred in the cabin with the door shut. Sam ran the start with John following on the card, and the air conditioning came on with the engine.

They were already at the west end of the field, so it was a short taxi to runway 7 Right. Sam announced on the tower frequency out of habit. "Phoenix Tower, Pilatus Six One Two Papa Romeo, departing 7 Right, northwest bound." The display read 41 Celsius. He told John to expect a longer roll in the heat, and they came off a little under halfway down the runway. He turned left over Tempe and climbed out to the northwest.

Passing ten thousand feet the vents were blowing cold. John looked back at Catherine. She had one hand up in front of a vent and her hair had dried.

"When do we get there?" she asked.

"About 20:25 Pacific," Sam said. "The sun's down around 19:55, so there'll be some light left."

"How far does this go?"

John told her. Ninety-five hundred aircraft in the air at 2:00 a.m. Eastern on Tuesday, September 5, no departures anywhere after 01:59, the last one off ADS-B at 19:02 yesterday, and Sam's the only one that landed. Ships holding their courses until they hit a coast. Every news feed stopped a few minutes before two. Distress beacons coming on at the same minute in Korea, Queensland and Bavaria. The grid going down one piece at a time, the East just before one this morning and the West a few hours later.

"Two o'clock Eastern is eleven here," she said.

"Yes."

"I laid down at ten-thirty and my alarm went off at eleven-thirty. Sam, where were you?"

Sam told it the way he would have told an investigator. He went on break over Indiana at twelve-thirty with Theresa and Ruth in the seats. His alarm went off at 03:15, and nobody answered the keypad. He got in on the emergency code and found them dead, put on the mask, called every frequency he had, and turned south for Edmonton. He landed at Edmonton, found Jack in his bunk, and walked the cabin. Three hundred and six passengers and thirteen cabin crew.

"Was the cabin pressure normal?" Catherine asked.

"Sixty-one hundred feet, no warnings."

"You were up in the bunk without a mask?"

"Yes."

"For how long?"

"Close to three hours."

"Did you feel anything when you woke up?"

"No. My alarm went off."

"John?"

"A thud woke me at five after two, something crashing a long way off," John said. "I went back to sleep. I felt fine in the morning."

"So did I." She was quiet for a while. "Sam, do you have family?"

"My wife and daughter. They are gone."

"I'm so sorry. What were their names?"

"Elena and Rosa. Rosa was twelve."

Nobody said anything for a few minutes.

"John, do you have anyone?"

"My wife died sixteen years ago. I have a brother, Mark. I haven't been able to reach him." He told her about Harry next door and the Pattersons and their two kids, and the two firefighters on the kitchen floor at Station 83.

"Who else is there?" she asked.

"Eleven posts on social media after two o'clock. One man in Porto wrote back and I haven't heard from him since. Then the 911 logs, over four thousand calls, and three people. You, the woman on the bridge, and somebody in Huntsville, Alabama."

"Who's in Huntsville?"

"I don't know. Sixteen calls and an address, no recording."

"Sixteen. You flew right past them."

"Port Angeles is hurt and we needed a doctor," Sam said.

Catherine didn't say anything to that.

"What are you going to do about Huntsville?" she asked a little later.

"We haven't decided," Sam said. "Port Angeles first."

"John, what do you do, that you can read 911 logs?"

"I'm a systems architect at Google. My team builds AI models for the FAA, the Coast Guard, FEMA, and I have access to their data."

"Does that include 911?"

"No. I had the AI hack the systems, a couple days ago I would be headed to prison." He turned back and smiled at her.

"What do you know about what did this?"

"Not much. It happened everywhere in the same few minutes, and it only killed people. I don't know what it is or if it could happen again."

"What about Palo Verde?"

"It should have shut itself down, they all did. The problem is the spent fuel pools. Once the backup power runs out they have ten to twenty-one days before the fuel is exposed. I wouldn't plan on going back to Phoenix."

They were over Nevada by then. She asked for the transcript again and John passed his phone back, and she read it through.

**20:05 PDT**

Seattle went by off the right wing with no lights in it. The sun was down behind the Olympics and the sky over the ridges had gone orange. Sam started down across Puget Sound and came up the Strait with the dark town of Port Angeles ahead and the long curve of the Hook out in the water.

**20:25 PDT**

William R. Fairchild International was on a bench above the water just west of town. Sam keyed the mic seven times on the airport frequency for the runway lights and nothing came on. "Port Angeles traffic, Pilatus Six One Two Papa Romeo, low pass runway 8." He flew it at two hundred feet with the landing lights on. Half a dozen deer were feeding along the edge of the pavement and two were standing on the centerline. They ran for the trees when he went over.

He came around and landed long, past where they had been, and taxied to the terminal. When John opened the door the air coming in was in the fifties and smelled like salt water and fir. Dan went down the airstair and stood on the ramp with his nose up.

Citizen Air was in the terminal, closed and locked. John cupped his hands against the glass and put his headlamp on it. Nobody was at the counter and a pegboard of keys hung on the wall behind it. Sam took the fire extinguisher off its bracket by the fuel tanks and put it through the door. The ring tagged CREW CAR was on the second row, and the car was a white sedan in the short-term lot, full tank.

Catherine looked in the back seat. "I can't lay her flat in that. What else is on this field?"

John pointed his headlamp down the apron. A red and white helicopter sat on a pad in front of a hangar with a Life Flight sign on it.

"That'll do," she said. "They'll have a stretcher."

They drove the sedan down the apron. The side door of the hangar was unlocked. A pilot sat in a recliner in the crew room and a woman in a flight suit was at the table with her head on her arms, and a man in the same flight suit lay on a bunk through the open door behind them. It smelled the way Cutter had, but not as bad in the cool. Catherine looked at each of them with her headlamp and then went past them into the supply room. She came out with a folding stretcher and handed it to Sam, went back for an orange drug box and a bag of IV fluids, and sent John to look for keys.

A hook by the crew room door held a Toyota key. Behind the hangar he pressed the button on it and the lights blinked on a gray 4Runner. He folded the back seats down and it was long enough for the stretcher. It started on the first try and the gauge read a little over half.

**20:50 PDT**

It was nearly dark when they got the stretcher and the drug box and Catherine's bags loaded. Catherine rode in the back with the gear and Dan, and Sam got up front. John turned on the headlights and drove out the airport gate.
