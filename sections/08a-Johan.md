# Johan Brandt

### 9/7/2028, 11:50 ship's time

The sun came through at ten minutes to noon and Johan took the sextant out on the starboard bridge wing. He got his altitude through a break in the overcast and worked it at the chart table: 51°58' north. The GPS said 51°59'. He wrote both in the log.

He slept on a mattress from the pilot's cabin, on the deck beside the radar, half an hour at a time, with the radar's guard zone set at twelve miles and the AIS alarm on. The first night he had kept the watch alarm keyed on at its longest setting, and it had woken him every twelve minutes until his hand wasn't steady on the parallel rule. On Wednesday night he turned the key to off. He wrote that in the log too, with the time.

F.O. SETTLING TANK LOW LEVEL came up on the panel at ten past two. He took the elevator down to the control room. Alvarez's logbook lay open on the desk where he had left it Tuesday, because he hadn't wanted to close it, and now he sat down in the chair and read it.

*1835 No. 2 F.O. purifier vibration high, changed over to No. 1. Will open No. 2 when*

He turned back a page, and another. Every morning a little after nine somebody had written *transferred F.O. from No. 3 P to settling*, with a figure in tonnes. There was no such entry for Tuesday, or Wednesday, or today. In twenty-six years of fire drills and abandon-ship drills nobody had ever drilled him on a fuel transfer.

It took him an hour and a half with the piping diagram off the bulkhead and a flashlight, in forty-five degrees on the purifier flat, following the line back from the pump to the bunker tank valve, tag by tag, before he was sure enough to open anything. He started the pump and stood at the level gauge until the figure began to climb. Then he went back to the desk and wrote it in Alvarez's book, under the unfinished line: time, tank, tonnes, and his name.

He came up at half past five with his shirt stuck to him and the AIS alarm going. *Ocean Prosper*, a bulk carrier, eleven miles off the port bow and crossing, closest point 0.2 miles in thirty-one minutes. He called her twice on 16 by name.

By the rules she was to keep clear of him, and he was to hold his course and speed. He held them with the glasses on her until she was two miles off. He gave her five short blasts on the whistle, which shook the windows, and she came on with her bow wave steady and the sun on her wheelhouse glass. He took the autopilot off and put the helm over twenty degrees to starboard, and it was most of a minute before the heading began to move.

She crossed astern of him at nine-tenths of a mile. There was nobody on her bridge wing. He brought the *Pacific Tender* back to 085, put the autopilot in, and logged it: time, range, name, and that she did not answer.
