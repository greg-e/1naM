# John Hartman

### 12:00

An Airbus A350-900 operated by Delta Air Lines had departed Atlanta, filed for Seoul, South Korea, and landed at Edmonton, Alberta, at 05:00 EDT. A Pilatus PC-12 had departed Edmonton at 07:31, no destination filed, its Flight ID **DAL27PDK**, squawking **7700**, general emergency.

"Give me full details of **DAL27PDK** PC-12 departed Edmonton at 07:31."

**Arrived Minneapolis-St. Paul 10:45 EDT. Departed Minneapolis-St. Paul 11:30 EDT.**

It was 12:10. The plane had left Minneapolis forty minutes ago.

"Have any of the other 351 aircraft diverted from their flight path after 2am EDT?"

**No.**

"Show the live track of **DAL27PDK**."

The PC-12 was passing through 8,000 feet, heading southeast.

"DAL27PDK," John said out loud. He looked back at the table. The A350 that had landed in Edmonton was Delta flight 27. Maybe a pilot from DAL27 was now flying this PC-12, and PDK was the destination. John knew PDK, it was Peachtree DeKalb Airport, where he spent most weekends maintaining or flying his Cessna 182.

"Nautical miles from Edmonton to PDK?"

**1,700 nm.**

"What is the range of the Pilatus PC-12?"

**About 1,500 nm with reserves and a load.**

So maybe he stopped in Minneapolis-St. Paul for fuel.

"Distance from Minneapolis-St. Paul to PDK and PC-12 flight time?"

**780 nm. 2 h 47 min at 280 kt.**

"If that's where they're headed, that puts them on the ground between 14:15 and 14:45," John said.

He pulled out his notebook and noted next to questions 1 and 3:
- *1. Where else?* **global**
- *2. How did this happen?*
- *3. Is there anyone else alive?* **yes**
- *4. Why am I alive?*

On a Saturday the drive to PDK took about 35 minutes. Today he had no idea, so he left right away.

He put his laptop and charger in his flight bag and took the small power station too, enough to charge the laptop a couple of times.

He took Georgia Highway 400 south, as he had that morning. The fire on the east side had spread to the whole retail center, and he could feel the heat as he drove by. He stopped slowing down and looking into the cars he passed. It took him 40 minutes to travel to the interchange with Interstate 285, double the normal time. He took the ramp onto 285 east, where there were more trucks. They lay jackknifed and flipped across the lanes, and in a couple of spots, even with four lanes, there looked to be no way through. Past the Chamblee-Dunwoody bridge black smoke rose from what looked like both sides of the interstate. He exited onto Chamblee-Dunwoody and went south, the back way to PDK he'd used many times when 285 was backed up.

His phone was already paired to the truck. He opened the public Gemini app and started talking.

John dictated: "I woke up this morning and everyone is dead. I went out looking around the neighborhood, I went for a drive and there's cars just abandoned on the road with dead people in them. There's fires everywhere. What do I do?"

**John, I'm here, and I'll help you through this. Please call 911 now. If you can't get through, go to the nearest emergency room or fire station. If you have a headache, dizziness, or nausea, get out into fresh air; carbon monoxide can change what you see and how you think. Is anyone with you? If you're feeling overwhelmed, you can call or text 988 any time.**

He knew that answer. His team had tuned ones like it. He closed the app and opened the connection to the dev box at home, his team's build, the one he'd used that morning on the flights.

"Everyone I've seen today is dead," he said. "What should my priorities be?"

**What do you need to know first?**

The notebook was open on the passenger seat. "Four things. Where else did it happen. How did it happen. Is anyone else alive. Why am I alive."

1. **Where else: check each region for normal activity, starting where it's daytime now. Traffic cameras, transit, flights, ship tracks, posts. Log what stopped and when. Log sources that don't answer separately.**
2. **How: not answerable from here. Save when each source stopped, to the second where it's recorded.**
3. **Anyone else alive: 911 records, card transactions, public posts.**
4. **Why you: not answerable yet. Record where each person found was and what they were doing at 02:00.**

John went down the list.

"One. Keep power, network, and service separate. A camera can have power and a connection and still show nobody doing anything. I want to know which layer quit."

**Three views per region: power, network, service.**

"Two. Working cutoff 02:00 Eastern; widen it if the data says to. Keep the history from before the cutoff for comparison. And save this. On Old Milton Parkway this morning I found a man dead in his car in a ditch. His fingers were pink. The dogs in my neighborhood are fine."

**Saved. Avoid contact with bodies and fluids until the cause is known.**

"Three. I called 911 twice this morning, around 7:40 from Old Milton and again around 9:10 from 400. An AI agent answered both times and said it was logging the call. Find out which counties and vendors hold the records and what access each needs. Research only for now. When you search, my two calls are the check. If they're not in what you find, tell me."

**Checks: two 911 calls, Fulton County, ~07:40 and ~09:10.**

"Card transactions. I bought gas at the QuikTrip at exit 8 on 400, somewhere around nine, nine-thirty. That's the check on those."

**Check: card purchase, QuikTrip, GA-400 exit 8, 09:00–09:30.**

"Posts. Put one up on my accounts now. My name, Atlanta, the date and time. No home address. Save every reply with its time and link."

**Posting.**

"All of this goes away when the power does."

**Then capture first. Save raw records from every source that answers, now and hourly. Pull whatever history each source keeps. Copy everything to the external drive.**

"And the house. Whatever can hurt me before I get back. I'm driving from Alpharetta to PDK, Peachtree DeKalb, to meet that PC-12."

**Water supply. Fires and gas leaks between Alpharetta and PDK. Nuclear plants, each by site. Offline copies of maps, charts, and medical references.**

At the light at Peachtree Industrial he said, "Read it back."

**Search every region for people and normal activity. Archive everything that answers, hourly, with history. Track power, network, and service status. Research the 911 records; checks are your two calls and the QuikTrip purchase. Post on your accounts, save replies. Water, hazards, nuclear plants by site. References offline.**

"Split it up and run it in parallel. Keep everything there, copies on the external drive. I'll go through it when I get home, including what you couldn't reach."

**Understood.**

"Get to work."
