# The Changed Field

### 10/6/2028, 10:20 local Pacific Time - John

John found the airport at the north end of Skaha Lake, with the runway nearly in line with the water and the town beyond it. He put a finger on the chart, checked the canal between the lakes, and looked out again. Sam had already started down.

They had brought the Pilatus. Sam wanted its climb and carrying room for the crossing, and enough fuel to search and return without finding a working pump at Penticton. John had the Cessna's search binder on his knees, with a clean sheet clipped over the last route. There were blankets, food, water and a tool bag in the cabin behind him. Neither of them had brought Dan.

The western ridges had been clear when they crossed. John had watched the cloud below their level rather than the mountains for a while, checking whether the gaps behind them were closing, until Sam told him to keep track of the ground as well. He had put the chart on his knees and followed their position until the land opened into the valley.

Sam called on the published airport frequency. Nobody answered. They passed where they could look down the runway, then flew on without landing. John photographed the apron and the road from the terminal, including the junction where it met the highway.

North of the airport, the city filled the strip between the two lakes. John kept the camera down long enough to look at the streets. Cars stood at intersections and along the curbs. There were boats in yards and apartment balconies with chairs on them. He couldn't see into the balconies they passed behind.

They followed the east side of Okanagan Lake, keeping the shoreline on the chart and looking up the roads that climbed away from it. Rows of trees and vines ran across the benches above the water. There were open patches between them, some beside houses and others reached by tracks that disappeared into the rows.

John took the wide views first. He had taped a pencil to the camera strap and stopped after each group to mark where it belonged.

One property had an open grass field beside a shed, with three pale rectangles lying against its wall and a blue barrel near the door. The house stood above it, at the end of a short lane off the road. John took a photograph because the field was big enough for somebody to put a signal out. He kept the house, the lane and the bend in the road in the frame.

Sam asked what he had seen.

"A place to look again," John said. "Nothing moving."

At the next road they turned back. John had used nearly all the margin beside the route and was writing down the sides of the page. He put the camera away while Sam brought them around, then took it up again for the return along the same shore.

They saw nobody outside. John checked the grass field once more as it passed behind the wing. The pale pieces were still beside the shed.

### 13:40 local Pacific Time

Tyler was at the wardroom laptop when they got back. He had a game open and clicked it shut as John came through the door.

"You can leave it," John said.

"Did you find anybody?"

"Not today."

The boy pulled his chair aside to make room. John put the camera on the table, took his jacket off and went to the galley for water before he sat down. He had worn the headset long enough to have a sore place behind one ear.

Sam brought the route sheet in. He had written *No landing* beside Penticton and put a line through the space for fuel taken aboard. Johan read the sheet before he took it to Catherine.

John copied the photographs into a new folder. Tyler went through them with him, the airport, the roads, the houses above the lake. He stopped at a boat on a trailer and asked if the man could have gone out in it.

"There could be another boat," John said. "I don't know."

Tyler went back to the wide picture. John enlarged the lane beside the grass field until he could follow it from the road to the house. He checked it against the chart, then made a print of the whole property. Sam came back with two bowls from the galley and moved the camera so he could set one down.

"Keep that one," he said. "It's easy to pick out."

John wrote the date on the white edge, marked the direction they had been looking, and put it in the binder. Tyler moved the mouse back to the airport photograph.

### 10/10/2028, 10:15 local Pacific Time - John

The pale rectangles were in the field.

John had the earlier print on his knees, held down by the binder clip. He looked from the shed in the photograph to the one below them. The blue barrel was at the door, but the three pieces were laid out away from the wall, with their long edges parallel and a space between each one.

"There," he said. "The white pieces."

Sam kept the airplane steady while John took a photograph. By the time he lowered the camera, the shed was passing under the wing.

"Those were against the wall," John said.

"I see them."

A dark shape moved near the house. John leaned toward the window and lost it behind a tree. He could see a shadow under the porch, a bush beside the steps, and nothing he could separate from either one.

"There might be somebody at the house."

"Where?"

"Porch. I can't get it now."

Sam brought them around for another look, with room to turn clear of the rising ground. John had the camera ready before the field came back into view. The pieces were still in their row. He got the porch and the windows, then the lane, and took his hand off the shutter.

He couldn't find the movement again.

They flew on toward the airport. John put the two images beside each other on the camera's screen, first the old one, then the new. Sam glanced across but kept his eyes outside.

"Let's come back once more," Sam said. "Give them time to get out."

"We've been over twice."

"They might need longer."

John looked back toward the field. "If that's somebody, they know we're here. We could land and go up."

"You saw something move. You didn't see a person."

"I know."

Sam made the airport call and listened. John took the photograph of the lane out of the binder. There was a branch off the main road, then the short drive past the shed. He could follow it without going over the hill behind the property.

"The pieces have moved," he said. "We have a road. We can get to that gate and see whether somebody answers."

Sam looked at the camera again when they were level.

"And if we can't get a car?"

"We come back to the airplane."

"If the road doesn't go where you think it does?"

"Same thing."

Sam gave the field ahead of them another look, then checked the time on the panel.

"We need to be leaving here by two. Earlier if the crossing starts to close."

John wrote it on the corner of the photograph. Sam reached over and tapped the paper.

"That's leaving. Not starting to drive back."

"I know."

Sam took them past the runway for a closer look. There were no aircraft on it and no vehicles across either end. John looked along the pavement and the taxiway as they passed. The airfield lights were off. Sam brought them around, called again, and landed in daylight on the runway they had just inspected.

They taxied clear and stopped on the apron away from the terminal. After shutdown, Sam walked around the airplane while John got the tool bag, their water and the photograph. He put their names, the day's date and *EDIZ HOOK, PORT ANGELES* on a sheet inside the cabin window. Under it he wrote where they were going.

At the Hook, Johan had Penticton and the search route, not this drive. He was there with Catherine, Della and Tyler. They had no working radio link from the apron to the ship. John put the marine handheld in the bag anyway, for when they were back within reach.

### 11:45 local Pacific Time

The rental sedan smelled of upholstery cleaner when John opened it. Sam had found the tagged keys in a locked drawer behind the rental counter and opened the drawer with the flat bar from the tool bag. They matched the tag to the license plate, checked the tires and looked under the hood before Sam tried the key.

The engine turned over and started. John watched the fuel gauge rise while Sam tested the brakes before they left the parking space. They drove to the edge of the lot and stopped again while John unfolded the road map.

They went north through town, then turned toward the road along the east side of the lake. John had the map open against the photograph. From the air he had seen the top of a low building with a green roof at the junction; from the passenger seat he found the roof only after they had gone by it. Sam stopped at the next place wide enough to turn and came back.

They were slower on the smaller road. There were cars half onto the shoulder, a branch sticking into the lane and places where John had to wait until they had passed a hedge to see the next drive. The lake showed between houses on their left. Once he asked Sam to stop so he could get out and look at the bend behind them.

He found the shed by its roof and the blue barrel, not the house.

"That's it," he said, getting back in. "Next drive."

Sam pulled off the road before the gate. The house was higher than John had thought, with a retaining wall below the front steps. The grass field lay beside the lane. He could see the three white doors through the fence now, laid flat with the dark handle holes facing up.

John left the photograph on the seat. Sam took the keys out and put them in his pocket, and they got out with the bag. John checked the time before they went to the gate.

There was no padlock. A wire loop held it to the post. Beyond it the lane went past the shed and up to the house, and the gravel beside the shed had a damp patch where water had run across it.

Sam called hello. He stood clear of the gate so the house could see both of them. John could hear the car's exhaust ticking behind him.

The side door opened and a woman came onto the step. She wore a brown jacket over a sweater, with the sleeves pushed back, and held a plastic bucket against her leg. She looked at the two of them, then down the lane toward the sedan.

John lifted a hand. "We're from the airplane. I'm John. This is Sam."

She put the bucket down without taking her eyes off them.

"I saw you last week," she said.

"We saw the doors," John said.

The woman came down the steps and walked past the shed. She had to pull the wire loop up with both hands to get it over the post. Sam held the gate while she opened it.
