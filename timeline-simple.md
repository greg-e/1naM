## 1. John — Day 0 — Alpharetta, GA

Wakes into the silence and works it, data on data, until the innocent explanations are all gone.

- Woken 2:05 AM by a distant boom; goes back to sleep. Wakes at 6 to a dead-still neighborhood. Widower — wife Naomi died 16 years ago.
- Walks Dan. Runs and discards explanations: work-from-home, a forgotten holiday (no — Monday was Labor Day). No kids at the 7:15 bus stop. Then the missing traffic noise pulls him out to Old Milton Parkway.
- The **"Ummm" device** starts here — his flat working hum for a problem that hasn't resolved into data.
- On the walk (~7:40 AM) he finds a dead driver in a car nosed into the ditch, engine still idling — checks for a pulse (Waffle-House-grill-line composure), finds none, shuts the engine off.
- Calls 911 from the roadside; a model-backed triage agent answers — he recognizes it as a deployment he's sat in rooms arguing over — and he can't get past its script to a person. (Seeds the Section 7 nationwide 911 search.)
- Doesn't turn back: checks a contractor's truck stopped in the median (also dead — doesn't even open the door; "data, not coincidence"), then walks to where Old Milton Parkway passes **under GA-400** and sees a jackknifed 18-wheeler hanging off the elevated deck, tractor through the rail over the parkway. That turns him around.
- Walks Dan home. Frozen news feeds, timestamps clustered before 2 AM = not local. Then the other silence — no aircraft all morning.
- Opens FlightAware: empty airspace, ~50 flights worldwide, several holding headings out to sea past their destinations (FedEx 767 etc. — "reached the flight decks at the same time, everywhere"). One exception: a Delta out of Atlanta he watches make a deliberate, controlled landing at Gander, NL and taxi in — one competent person still alive and acting. Knows Gander is *the* transatlantic diversion field, knows the run flies an augmented crew.
- DOT traffic cameras — "it went everywhere." 9 AM work sync: no one joins ("horror was mostly the arithmetic finishing"). Calls everyone he knows (brother Mark first); every line rings out.
- Ends alone with the dog: the working hum "gets words in it" — his first cry to God since Naomi died. "Oh, God. Please help me."

## 2. Sam — Day 0 — Delta 84 (ATL→CDG, A330-900neo) → Gander International, NL

The sole surviving pilot discovers the flight deck dead and diverts.

- 2:00 AM Eastern: First Officer and Relief Pilot die at the controls while Sam is resting (crew ran two rest periods, not three).
- Aircraft flies itself for ~4 hours. Sam comes up for his handoff ~5:45 AM and finds them dead, ~500 nm ENE of Newfoundland.
- Declares an emergency, turns for **St. John's** (nearest suitable field). The Avalon coast is **zero-zero in fog** — two ILS approaches to minimums, two missed approaches, won't gamble a blind autoland at an unmanned field.
- Diverts **~100 nm inland to Gander** (out from under the fog), orbits once, low inspection pass, lands solo with no ATC — **touchdown ~8:25 AM Eastern**, the landing John watches live on FlightAware.
- Dark terminal, scattered dead (fewer than a daytime event — 2:00 AM timing).
- Tries Elena, his mother, a squadron buddy, his sister on a weak signal — voicemail or endless ringing, no answers.

## 6. Sam — Day 0 — Gander (terminal, main ramp, GA/FBO apron — PAL Aviation)

Grief, then the unglamorous work of getting an aircraft flying alone.

- Sam breaks down fully once the call ends, then moves on training and discipline alone.
- Grabs water, food, and a live phone charger from the dead terminal first.
- Walks past his own dead A330; weighs only King Airs vs. PC-12s on the GA apron; picks a PC-12 for simplicity. (Fixed-wing only — no helicopter considered.)
- Getting it flying is logistical, not physical: FBO break-in for keys off a tagged pegboard; a second GROUND EQUIPMENT key ring solves the card-locked fuel bowser; he hand-fuels an aircraft himself for the first time in his career (PC-12 is low-wing — just a step stool).
- Grid power and phone signal are still on in Newfoundland (hydro grid) — he pulls real weather and does a genuine flight plan.
- Plans two legs (Gander → Bangor before dark, overnight, then Bangor → PDK) but never totals how much ground time ate his daylight.
- Notices only at departure, rationalizes ("Bangor will have lights"), launches into fading light anyway.

## 7. John — Day 0–1 — Anthropic Midtown office → REI Atlantic Station → PDK

Invents the nationwide 911 search and gets a satellite link working.

- Finally makes the deferred office visit — empty, real backup power, dead building internet, a tetherable cell signal he knows won't last. Eats/hydrates first (mirrors Sam in Section 6).
- Epiphany: his own Section 1 911 call means every survivor who called 911 left the same trace.
- Runs many parallel model instances against dispatch systems nationwide overnight; sifts ~4,000 calls down to **three hits with address + phone: Huntsville AL, Phoenix AZ, Portland OR**.
- His cell connection dies completely ~noon Wednesday before he can call any of them.
- Packs a mobile kit (three laptops, docks, cables, a case), decides he's not returning to Midtown.
- Detours to REI at Atlantic Station, finds a display satellite hotspot; the model helps activate it via a dormant manufacturer demo credential (explicitly hypothetical).
- Places calls as VoIP over the hotspot: **Huntsville and Phoenix fail outright** (network down). **Portland answers — Grace**, injured (believes her leg is broken), alone at a coastal overlook given by coordinates.
- Reasons the Pacific Northwest hydro grid is why Portland is still reachable. Promises to call Grace back.
- Departs for PDK mid-afternoon with the full kit; never returns to Midtown.

## 8. Sam — Day 0–1 — Gander → Bangor vicinity → Bar Harbor → Augusta → Loring/Limestone, ME

The daylight miscalculation comes due; the US grid collapse has reached Maine.

- Reaches Bangor after full dark expecting a lit field. Bangor is completely dark — no response to pilot-controlled-lighting radio clicks. Power itself is out.
- Tries Bar Harbor and Augusta State — both dark, no response, each diversion burning fuel margin.
- From his Air Force background, thinks of Loring AFB / Limestone — a decommissioned SAC base likely to have hardened power. It works.
- Lands safely, exhausted and relieved, now much further north than planned. New Limestone→PDK distance problem unsolved.

## 9. Sam — Day 1 — Loring/Limestone → Bangor → en route to PDK

Solves the fuel problem by backtracking; the range risk resolves with it.

- Wakes at first light; confirms Limestone has no accessible Jet-A (checks the fuel farm, two hangars — decommissioned decades ago).
- Fuel math also confirms he couldn't make the full Limestone→PDK distance anyway.
- Backtracks ~150 nm to Bangor — daylight makes last night's dark-runway problem irrelevant. Bangor was always suitable, just unusable after dark.
- Refuels easily: self-serve card lot with a manual hand-crank pump, ~40 min, no drama. This also resolves the Section 8 range risk — Bangor→PDK is the original safe distance.
- Tries calling John from the ramp — no signal at all.
- Departs Bangor for PDK direct, mid-morning, real fuel margin, hours of buffer before 4 PM.

## 10. John — Day 1 — PDK (DeKalb-Peachtree Airport)

## 11. John — Day 1–2 — Huntsville International → Tyler's house → a Waffle House → back to the ramp

Tyler is rescued; John's composure breaks in front of someone for the first time (he broke down privately once already, Section 1).

- Sam does his standard low pass. They get a car from the airport rental lot (keys in it, full tank, first-try start) and drive in past patchy working traffic signals and porch lights — Huntsville's grid is up in patches (TVA's heavily hydro mix).
- At the house: Tyler's father David dead in the living room (no struggle). Tyler locked in his bedroom since finding the body; lets them in after they talk him through the door.
- Tyler called 911 repeatedly and already knows about his father.
- Waffle House, lit by a live patch of grid — both employees confirmed dead but outside in their cars on a break, so the interior is untouched. Sam cooks a real meal for the first time in his life (learned from videos with his daughter).
- Tyler talks about his mother and the ISS without asking directly if she's alive; John promises they'll figure it out, not tonight.
- Back at the aircraft, once Tyler's asleep, **John breaks down in front of Sam** — the first witnessed time (he broke privately on Day 0, Section 1) — over Tyler's grief, not his own losses. Sam sits with him wordlessly.
- They sleep in shifts in the cabin (Day 1 into Day 2, Thursday Sep 7) and depart next morning for Phoenix — Sam flags "a fuel stop, probably two," not yet planned.

## 12. John — Day 2 — Huntsville → Amarillo, TX (fuel stop) → Phoenix Sky Harbor (KPHX)

The fuel math works out to one stop; Catherine finds them.

- Sam works real fuel math: direct Huntsville→Phoenix (~1,285 nm, FL280, ~5h05m against a headwind) needs more fuel than the PC-12 PRO carries — a genuine shortfall.
- One stop at Amarillo, TX solves it cleanly (Amarillo sits on the direct routing) — not two, as guessed in Section 11.
- En route, Sam hands John a full cruise segment to fly solo under supervision. Tyler asks whether the ISS is visible from altitude (it isn't; Sam promises to help him spot it another night).
- Amarillo stop is quick and routine (self-serve pump, ~40 min); John fuels the aircraft himself for the first time.
- Sam still flies a low pass into the much larger Sky Harbor field.
- On landing, **Catherine Reyes** — staying near a Phoenix hospital since Day 0 — hears the engine (first working engine she's heard in three days) and **drives her car through the airport perimeter fence** onto the ramp to reach them before they finish taxiing.
- She's the Phoenix hit John could never identify. Joins the group.

## 13. John — Day 2 — Phoenix → Portland (PDX) → coastal overlook near Seaside/Cannon Beach → Providence Seaside Hospital → Grace's coastal house

Grace is rescued; the original mission completes; TruckHouse gets its first mention.

- Catherine is briefed on Grace and commits immediately ("Absolutely").

- Car from the PDX rental lot to Grace's coordinates: found self-splinted with driftwood, alone for three days, doubting the Section 7 phone call was real.
- Catherine treats her at the real, still-powered but unstaffed Providence Seaside Hospital — a clean fibula fracture, casted, portable X-ray confirming it. Her first on-page medical scene.
- Grace's own coastal house still has power and hot water; everyone showers for the first time since the event.
- John cooks a full dinner from a still-stocked local market (parallel to Sam's Section 11 meal).
- Over dinner, **Catherine mentions TruckHouse for the first time** — her brother's overland-vehicle shop near Reno, NV — and raises going there eventually, status unknown. Treated as a future goal, not urgent.
- **This completes the Huntsville → Phoenix → Portland mission.** Traveling group: John, Sam, Tyler, Catherine, Grace, Dan.
